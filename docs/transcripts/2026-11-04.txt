Good morning. It's Wednesday, 4 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Democrat James Talarico flipped a US Senate seat in Texas on Tuesday, defeating Republican Ken Paxton. Texas had not elected a Democratic senator since 1988 before this win. Talarico, a 37-year-old former schoolteacher and Presbyterian seminarian, credited ordinary Texans for helping him overcome powerful political and financial interests.

Democrat Gina Hinojosa has defeated Republican Governor Greg Abbott, ending over 30 years of Republican control in the Texas governor's office. Hinojosa, 52, an Austin state representative, will take office in January 2027. She will be the first Latina to serve as Texas governor and the first Democratic governor since Ann Richards, who left office in 1995. Hinojosa's campaign focused on public education, household costs, and criticism of the state's Republican leadership. She opposed the private-school voucher program introduced by Abbott in 2026, which allocated $1 billion in tax money for students attending private schools.

Max Miller has been ousted from Congress following domestic abuse allegations made by his estranged wife, Emily Moreno. Moreno accused Miller of holding a gun to her head, scalding her with boiling water, and causing their toddler daughter to suffer a broken collarbone. Miller denies these allegations and has filed a defamation lawsuit against Moreno. In response, he released documents and audio recordings to dispute the claims and defended himself during a social media livestream. Meanwhile, a House ethics committee is investigating Miller over allegations of domestic violence and drug use. Democrat Brian Poindexter defeated Miller in the recent election and will take the congressional seat next year.

David Jones is set to enter administration after last-minute negotiations between its private equity owner and main landlord failed. The department store has not paid rent since September, breaching its lease agreements. Scentre Group, which owns Westfield and is David Jones's primary landlord, made a generous offer to Anchorage Capital Partners, the private equity owner, but it was declined. Administrators are expected to aim to keep stores open with minimal disruption during Black Friday and the Christmas period to secure essential revenue. Meanwhile, David Jones's chief executive, Erica Berchtold, has been engaged in advanced talks with key stakeholders regarding the company's future.

CrowdStrike has introduced a new kill switch that can shut down rogue artificial intelligence agents, the company's CEO George Kurtz announced on Tuesday. The AI Kill Switch, part of CrowdStrike's Falcon Guardian platform, enables security teams to terminate misbehaving AI agents, prevent their restart, and cut off their network access. This technology, available immediately, aims to address safety concerns amid the rapid adoption of AI agents in businesses. Kurtz highlighted the growing demand for CrowdStrike's security tools as companies increasingly deploy AI. He also referenced a partnership with Anthropic to protect its Claude Cowork product, noting that enterprise customers insisted on integrating CrowdStrike's technology before deploying Anthropic's AI offerings.

Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva has won Brazil's presidential election in 2026, ending seven consecutive conservative wins in Latin America since Donald Trump's return to office. Chinese President Xi Jinping congratulated Lula, describing China-Brazil relations as "a model of solidarity and cooperation among Global South countries." Meanwhile, the Trump administration granted US visas to Lula's defeated rival, Flavio Bolsonaro, and his family as they fled to Miami. During his tenure, Trump imposed punitive tariffs of up to 50 percent on Brazilian exports and sanctioned Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes. Brazil exported approximately $100 billion in goods to China last year, nearly three times its exports to the US. Additionally, a 190 billion yuan currency-swap line provides Brazil with a hedge against dollar weaponisation. Right-wing parties have secured majorities in both chambers of the incoming Brazilian Congress.

The mayor of Nice has announced an immediate reinforcement of security in local schools following an assault on a school director. Éric Ciotti plans to deploy 11 additional agents of public road surveillance and municipal police officers to monitor schools, particularly during student entry and exit times. The assault occurred on Monday, November 2, 2026, when a mother of a student attacked a school director and a teacher. The mother recorded part of the incident on her phone and later expressed regrets during a second interrogation while in custody. Authorities have noted that the suspect was subject to an Obligation to Leave French Territory and an administrative ban from the territory.

A recent study led by psychologist Dr Lena Glaser found that taking cold showers can improve both physical and mental health. Participants aged 18 to 65 were asked to lower their shower temperature for 12 weeks, with some going as cold as their shower would allow. Many reported feeling more awake and refreshed immediately after a cold shower, and those who continued experienced longer-term health benefits. On average, participants took 31 per cent fewer sick days than a control group. Additionally, reducing water temperature from 40°C to 15°C saved energy and cut CO2 emissions by 15 kilograms per person over the study period, equivalent to driving 100 kilometres. Dr Glaser noted that the environmental impact of cold showers is comparable to recycling.
