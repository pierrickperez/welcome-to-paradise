Good morning. It's Tuesday, 27 October. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Israel is holding a crucial election seen as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's leadership since the 2023 Hamas attacks. Polls suggest Netanyahu's coalition may lose its majority, but the opposition might also struggle to form a government. Former right-wing leaders Naftali Bennett and Avigdor Liberman could attempt to unseat Netanyahu. Meanwhile, a suspected stabbing attempt against opposition leader Yair Golan was foiled in Tel Aviv during a voter mobilisation event.

French football star Kylian Mbappé has apologised after Chinese brands dropped him over a joke calling Mao Zedong a dictator. The backlash led major sponsors like Dongpeng and Mengniu to suspend deals, while Chinese state media censored related content and La Liga broadcasts were halted. The Chinese Foreign Ministry condemned the remarks, sparking a widespread boycott and removal of Mbappé's endorsements in China.

A ceasefire has begun in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky to allow aid deliveries and civilian evacuations. The truce was reportedly secured through mediation involving US First Lady Melania Trump and Russia's Commissioner for Human Rights. Russian President Vladimir Putin accused the EU and NATO of prolonging the war during talks with Nordic leaders, including former Finnish Prime Minister Alexander Stubb and Norway's Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre. While the ceasefire is reportedly being respected, concerns remain over its durability and evacuation safety.

The Democratic Republic of Congo has reported over 10,000 Ebola cases and nearly 4,900 deaths since May 2026. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo virus, for which no approved vaccine or specific treatment exists, complicating containment efforts. Cases have spread to Kenya and Nigeria, with investigations ongoing. Clinical trials for antivirals and vaccines are underway but no approved options are currently available for this strain.

As the US midterm elections approach, Democrats hold a slight edge and are favoured to take control of the House, though the Senate race remains uncertain. President Donald Trump has intensified anti-Muslim attacks against Abdul El-Sayed, a Michigan Democratic Senate candidate who could become the first Muslim US senator. Meanwhile, Trump has labelled former FBI Director James Comey a 'dirty cop' amid new federal charges accusing Comey of sharing classified information in 2016; Comey denies any wrongdoing and calls the charges politically motivated. Trump is actively campaigning for Republican candidates in key states including Mississippi and Texas.

Jordan has carried out deadly airstrikes in Syria targeting Islamic State positions in response to an attack on a Jordanian church. Syrian state media report that at least seven civilians were killed in the strikes. The operation marks a significant escalation in Jordan’s efforts to combat Islamic State militants in the region.

South Africa has suspended its asylum application process following recent anti-migrant protests and violence. The unrest included calls for crackdowns on foreigners and undocumented migrants, with some incidents escalating to murder. The government’s move aims to address the rising tensions amid growing concerns over migration.

French MPs are set to vote on a Sánchez-style amnesty plan that would grant legal status to hundreds of thousands of undocumented immigrants. The measure is expected to receive support from presidential candidate Raphaël Glucksmann. This proposal could significantly alter France’s approach to immigration policy.

HSBC has reported a 32 percent rise in profits, despite its share price falling six percent today. This comes amid the UK facing its highest five-year debt yields since 2008 and record financial distress among smaller businesses, with 738,000 in significant trouble. The profit surge has reignited calls for new bank taxes ahead of the upcoming UK budget.

Boeing has narrowed its third-quarter loss to 116 million dollars, down from over five billion a year ago. Defence sales rose 15 percent to nearly eight billion dollars, offsetting a slight drop in commercial aircraft revenue. The company delivered 161 planes in the quarter and says production of the 737 Max is stabilising, with plans to increase output next year.

Journalists from Mediapart and Politis were assaulted during the Rassemblement National congress in France over the weekend. The attacks were reportedly carried out by the party’s security and militants. More than 40 journalist societies have condemned the violence, stressing the importance of press freedom in political reporting.

The European Commission has proposed reforms to Frontex, the EU’s border agency, including a permanent corps of up to 30,000 agents. A new 'Return Reserve' of 17,000 agents would be available to member states for rapid deployment. The plan also includes a Quick Reaction Force, an online border threat monitoring centre, and a deportation coordination hub.

Powerful king tides have caused flooding in Southern California and Florida, impacting homes and businesses. Residents and business owners have been forced to scoop water from their properties as floodwaters continue to threaten streets on both US coasts.

Italian parliamentary email systems, including the mailbox of Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, have been targeted in a mass hacking attack. Authorities say the attacks are ongoing but under control, with no emergency declared and protective measures in place alongside the National Cybersecurity Agency.

At the Starmus VIII Festival, astronauts Chris Hadfield and Victor Glover shared their spaceflight experiences. They highlighted the significance of the renewed space exploration efforts, known as the New Space race, emphasising its role in advancing science and humanity's future beyond Earth.

That's your global news briefing. Have a good day.
