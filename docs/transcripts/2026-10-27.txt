Good morning. It's Tuesday, 27 October. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Israeli voters participated in a general election on Tuesday, October 27, 2026, marking the country's first election since the Hamas attacks on October 7, 2023. These attacks resulted in around 1,200 deaths and over 250 abductions. The Palestinian Ministry of Health reports that Israel's subsequent military campaign in Gaza has caused more than 74,500 deaths. Voter turnout was notably high, reaching 16.68% by 10am local time, the highest at that hour since 1981, with over 1.2 million Israelis casting ballots, an increase compared to the previous election in 2022. By midday, turnout rose to 31.5%, the highest by that time since 1977. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, leader of the right-wing Likud party, is seeking to maintain his position amid this election. He urged right-wing voters to participate, describing the situation as an 'emergency hour' to prevent gains by left-wing parties.

Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre held a 90-minute phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin, which was described as frank and substantive. The Finnish and Norwegian leaders spoke solely on behalf of their own countries and did not represent Europe as a whole. During the conversation, Putin stated that a just and lasting peace in Ukraine would only be possible by fully considering Russia's interests. He also accused the European Union and NATO of prolonging the conflict through their confrontational policies. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Støre emphasized that Norway and Finland support Ukraine's right to self-defense. President Stubb highlighted that ending the war requires direct discussions. The extended call exceeded the initially allocated 20 minutes, reflecting the complexity of the issues discussed.

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo have now surpassed 10,000, with government data reporting 10,060 cases and 4,887 deaths. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo virus, was declared on May 15 and is noted for being the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in history, according to the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. This strain is rare and currently has no approved vaccine or specific treatment. Nearly half of those infected in the DRC have died. The virus has also spread beyond the DRC's borders, with Kenya confirming nine infections, including two deaths, after a patient travelled from the DRC through Uganda to Nairobi. Nigeria has reported an infection in a Swiss woman, the wife of a Swiss diplomat, who became ill after visiting Uganda. Health authorities continue to monitor the situation closely as the outbreak evolves.

Chancellor John Healey is set to announce new pay rates in the upcoming Budget on Wednesday. The National Minimum Wage for 18-to-20 year-olds is expected to increase by 3.9%, from £10.85 to approximately £11.27 per hour. This rise is smaller than the increases seen in the previous two years, when Labour raised the rate to fulfil a manifesto commitment. Meanwhile, the National Living Wage for older workers, currently £12.71 an hour, is also set to rise by 3.9% to £13.20. Additionally, the government will freeze fuel duty once again and maintain the 5p duty discount. Plans are also in place to remove import taxes on certain food items, which could save households up to £21 annually.

World Bank President Ajay Banga has stated that capital remains available despite concerns that artificial intelligence could push interest rates higher. Speaking to CNBC, Banga highlighted that the World Bank mobilised $112 billion last year, nearly three times the amount recorded three years earlier. He explained that emerging markets are not experiencing the same hesitancy because investments are primarily directed towards infrastructure projects such as bridges and roads. Banga also clarified that the private capital overseen by the World Bank does not go to governments. While he believes artificial intelligence will be transformative and profitable, he acknowledged that there will be challenges ahead.

Kylian Mbappé has apologised for comments he made about the late Chinese leader Mao Zedong, which led to several Chinese brands ending their partnerships with him. The footballer referred to a meme involving a 'Chinese dictator' during an interview on the French talk show The Bridge. Following the backlash, brands including Mengniu and Dongpeng severed ties with Mbappé. La Liga also issued an apology, expressing regret for the offence caused to Chinese fans. Mbappé said his comment was "ill-judged" and that he now understands it hurt many people in China and Chinese communities worldwide. China's foreign ministry spokesperson Lin Jian stated that words and deeds hurting the feelings of the Chinese people would face opposition from all sectors of Chinese society.

Democrats are currently favoured to gain control of the U.S. House in the upcoming midterm elections, holding an 8-point advantage among likely voters on the generic congressional ballot. President Trump's approval rating has seen a slight improvement, now sitting at 40%. Meanwhile, economic concerns remain significant, with one in three Americans facing financial hardships and four in ten reporting that their personal finances are worsening. Issues influencing voter priorities vary, with preserving democracy resonating strongly among Democrats and independents, while immigration is the top concern for MAGA supporters. These factors are shaping the political landscape as the midterms approach.

The European Commission has proposed a reform of Frontex, the EU agency responsible for external border control. The reform aims to make Frontex the main operational arm of the EU's migrant return policy. Under the proposal, the permanent corps of Frontex will be increased to 30,000 agents. Additionally, the Commission plans to create a 'Return Reserve' of 17,000 agents who will remain available to member states and can be deployed when needed. Other measures include establishing a Quick Reaction Force for crisis situations, an online monitoring centre to identify potential border threats, and a return coordination centre to streamline operations. These changes are intended to enhance the EU's capacity to manage its external borders and migrant returns more effectively.

Coca-Cola has raised its full-year earnings forecast for the third consecutive quarter, expecting comparable earnings per share to grow by at least 11%, up from a previous estimate of 9 to 10%. The company reported a third-quarter net income of $4.01 billion, or 93 cents per share, up from $3.7 billion, or 86 cents per share, a year earlier. Net sales increased by 7% to $13.4 billion, with organic revenue also rising 7%. Unit case volume grew 5% for the second quarter in a row, driven by demand outside North America, where volume was flat due to holiday timing and product shortages. Shares rose about 1% in premarket trading.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has called on European and Asian nations to find their place amid a changing global order. He stressed the need to assert interests in an international environment shaped by renewed great-power rivalry. Merz highlighted the stability offered by Germany and Europe as a counterpoint to unpredictable tariffs and trade restrictions. Europe aims to expand partnerships to increase free trade based on open competition, reliability, and a rules-based order. He also advocated for a new free-trade agreement between the EU and the Trans-Pacific Partnership. Merz and Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong agreed to enhance cooperation in security, trade, investment, sustainable energy, and artificial intelligence.

AI-driven Flock cameras, produced by an Atlanta-based company, are being installed across the US, raising concerns and sparking bipartisan, citizen-led campaigns ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. These cameras scan licence plates and record details such as location, time, car models, and bumper stickers. In Cherokee County, with around 260,000 residents, there have been notable cases of law enforcement officials misusing the technology. A September report by The Washington Post revealed that at least 100 police employees nationwide faced accusations of unauthorised use. Flock Safety states the cameras aid crime fighting by linking vehicle sightings and supporting investigations. Opposition is visible on social media, with videos showing how to block camera lenses and images of protest signs on the devices.
