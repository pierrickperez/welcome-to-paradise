Good morning. It's Tuesday, 27 October. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Voter turnout in Israel's election reached its highest level in decades, with 31.5% of Israelis casting their ballots by noon, marking the highest midday turnout since 1977. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu urged right-wing voters to participate, warning that abstaining could result in a government formed with parties representing Palestinian citizens of Israel. This election is the first since the Hamas attacks on October 7, 2023, and Israel's subsequent military campaign in Gaza. Additionally, tens of thousands of Israelis living abroad returned to the country to vote in this election.

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) have surpassed 10,000 as the outbreak continues to spread across the country and beyond its borders. Government data released on Tuesday put confirmed cases at 10,060 and deaths at 4,887. The outbreak, declared on May 15, is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a rare Ebola strain for which no vaccine or treatment has been approved. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) has said at least 95 percent of cases must come from the known contacts of infected people for the outbreak to be considered under control. Five months into the outbreak, responders in the DRC say some treatment centres are overwhelmed, while remote and newly affected areas are short of medical supplies and health workers. Kenya has confirmed nine infections, including two deaths, after a patient traveled from DR Congo through Uganda to the capital, Nairobi. Nigeria also confirmed an infection in a Swiss woman, the wife of a Swiss diplomat, who fell ill after visiting Uganda. Authorities are investigating where she contracted the virus. She was evacuated to Switzerland.

Finland and Norway held a direct phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin on 27 October 2026 to discuss ending the war in Ukraine. The call lasted 90 minutes, despite only 20 minutes initially being allocated. The leaders spoke solely on behalf of their own countries, not representing Europe or other nations. Discussions covered pressing issues such as attacks on energy infrastructure and the situation in the Black Sea. Norway’s Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finland’s President Alexander Stubb reiterated their support for Ukraine and stressed the importance of a diplomatic resolution to the conflict. Russian President Vladimir Putin stated that a "just and lasting peace" would require fully considering Russia's interests. The call followed earlier consultations involving Finland, Norway, Ukraine, and the US, including a meeting in Oslo on 20 October 2026. Denmark's Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen criticised Finland and Norway for not coordinating the call with other European allies.

French football star Kylian Mbappe has apologised for an "ill-judged" comment about a former Chinese leader that angered Chinese football fans and led to the loss of at least two sponsorships in China. The controversy began after a video circulated online in which Mbappe appeared to joke about a Chinese dictator, describing a meme featuring his face altered onto that of the leader as the funniest internet meme he had seen. Chinese companies Dongpeng Beverage, Inner Mongolia Mengniu Dairy, and cheesemaker Milkground subsequently ended their contracts with Mbappe. In a statement, Mbappe said, "My comment was ill-judged, and I now understand that it hurt many people in China and in Chinese communities around the world. I apologise to everyone my words have upset."

World Bank President Ajay Banga has stated that capital remains available despite concerns that artificial intelligence could push interest rates higher. Speaking to CNBC, Banga highlighted that the World Bank mobilised $112 billion last year, nearly three times the amount from three years ago. He explained that emerging markets are not experiencing the same hesitancy because investments are primarily directed towards infrastructure projects such as bridges and roads. Banga also noted that the private capital overseen by the World Bank does not go to governments, helping to avoid fears of debt crises and rising bond yields in some countries. While he believes AI will be transformative and profitable, Banga acknowledged there is uncertainty about future challenges.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has called on European and Asian nations to find their place amid a shifting global order marked by renewed great-power rivalry. Merz stressed the importance of asserting national interests within this environment. He highlighted the stability offered by Germany and Europe as a deliberate contrast to unpredictable tariffs and trade restrictions. Europe aims to broaden partnerships to promote free trade based on open competition, reliability, and a rules-based system. Merz advocated for a new free-trade agreement between the European Union and the Trans-Pacific Partnership. Additionally, Merz and Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong agreed to enhance cooperation in security, defence, trade, investment, sustainable energy, and artificial intelligence.

The French government considered measures to block social networks during last Saturday's protests. In the week leading up to October 24, the French interior ministry communicated with telecom operators to explore options for cutting access to social networks or mobile internet in specific areas. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu directly requested that French telecom operators be prepared to block access to platforms including TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, and X. These steps were part of the government's efforts to manage the situation during the protests.

Former FBI director James Comey has been federally charged for the third time over allegations of leaking national defence information. The charges relate to an alleged disclosure of classified material to the New York Times in October 2016. The criminal information was filed directly by a prosecutor in Washington DC. Comey has denied any wrongdoing, describing the charges as an "appalling abuse" in a video posted on Substack. The US Justice Department filed the charges to prevent the 10-year statute of limitations from expiring earlier this month. The charges accuse Comey of transmitting and conspiring to transmit national defence information to a reporter. Comey's lawyers have responded by filing a motion to dismiss, arguing the case is legally improper and may exceed the statute of limitations.
