Russian strikes have hit two major bridges in Kyiv, causing damage to critical infrastructure and disrupting heating and water supplies in parts of the capital. Kyiv's Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed that the North and South bridges were targeted and have since been closed to traffic. The attacks occurred late on Monday, with loud explosions heard in the city just before midnight on Tuesday. In response, Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge, which links Russia to annexed Crimea, for the second consecutive day. Traffic on the Kerch Bridge was suspended following the Ukrainian strike. Mayor Klitschko reported that some areas of Kyiv are experiencing interruptions to water and heating services as a result of the missile strikes.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy has warned shipping companies against using the U.S.-backed southern route through the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as fully closed and a "minefield of fire." On Tuesday, the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations reported that a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and subsequently caught fire. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy stated on Monday that two oil tankers had struck naval mines and caught fire while attempting to cross the southern part of the strait, a route Tehran considers unauthorised. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency separately reported two tanker attacks in the strait on Monday. These developments coincided with the arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt in the Gulf region on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington, as the US reinforced its military presence in the Middle East.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to join leaders from Australia, Japan, and the US for the Quad Leaders' Summit in Manila. Modi will also attend the ASEAN-India summit and the 21st East Asia Summit. This marks the first meeting between Modi and US President Donald Trump since the recent US midterm elections. However, no bilateral talks between Modi and Trump are expected during the summit. According to Indian news outlet The Print, a bilateral meeting is planned for next month in Florida during the G20 leaders' summit. President Trump will arrive in Manila on Tuesday, as the summit concludes, and is not anticipated to make any significant policy or diplomatic announcements. This is the second consecutive year President Trump is attending the annual gathering of Southeast Asian leaders, reflecting the United States' strategic interest in the region as a maritime counterbalance to China.

Teams representing Gabriel Attal, Bruno Retailleau, and David Lisnard are currently negotiating the details of a potential primary for the right and centre ahead of the 2027 presidential election. The discussions, held on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, involved the chiefs of staff from these three politicians, with Edouard Philippe's team notably absent, as he has repeatedly stated he will not participate. Retailleau and Attal, both currently weakened in the polls, are focusing on setting the date, organising debates, and minimising voting costs to encourage maximum participation. Meanwhile, Lisnard supports a broad primary that could include Eric Zemmour, with his supporters emphasising the goal of defeating Macronism and winning the primary. The call for this primary was made by Senate President Gérard Larcher on Saturday, November 14, 2026.

U.S. investigations into Megaspeed began in 2024 over potential export-control violations. In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list after Commerce Department investigators requested records concerning sales to the company. That same month, U.S. officials and Singapore police inspected Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang. Following this, the Department of Justice took the lead in the criminal investigation. In February 2026, Singapore barred Alice Huang from re-entering the country after authorities found she had knowingly provided inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed. Huang was reportedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to gather information on Jensen Huang, though the Justice Department has not concluded if she was a Chinese spy. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO last week amid revelations of multiple extramarital relationships. The Commerce Department also moved to close a loophole allowing Chinese companies access to Nvidia's advanced computing power through third-country data centres.

Deutsche Bank faced internal debates over handling Russian clients amid sanctions concerns. An email titled 'Russian Sanctions Urgent Request' was sent by an anti-financial crime specialist regarding these clients. The bank discussed which Russian clients they could unwind and whether to advise them to withdraw money before sanctions took effect. Among Deutsche Bank's clients were Russian billionaires Oleg V. Deripaska and Viktor F. Vekselberg. Despite repeated warnings from risk managers about the dangers of maintaining relationships with Russian oligarchs, the bank prioritised profits. This internal conflict highlights the challenges financial institutions face in balancing regulatory compliance with business interests.

Pesticides sold by European chemical companies were linked to over 1,600 poisoning reports in Brazil last year, according to an investigation. About a third of these cases involved chemicals banned in the European Union due to their risks to people and the environment. Of 2,857 poisoning reports naming an identifiable agricultural product, 1,605 cited brands sold by European pesticide firms. The investigation highlighted Reglone, named in 44 reports including four deaths. Overall, products sold by European companies were associated with 32 reported deaths. Syngenta products appeared in 425 reports, while BASF products were cited 179 times. The Brazilian health ministry recorded 75 cases involving diquat last year, a significant increase from 36 cases over the previous five years. Dormex, made in Germany by Alzchem and distributed by BASF in Brazil, was linked to 17 confirmed poisonings.

French President Emmanuel Macron is set to meet Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim in Kuala Lumpur on Tuesday to discuss enhancing defence, trade, energy, and critical minerals cooperation. This visit marks Macron's first to Malaysia since 2017 and precedes the 70th anniversary of diplomatic relations between the two nations. Malaysia's foreign ministry stated the talks aim to upgrade bilateral ties into a strategic partnership. The leaders will witness the signing of several memoranda of understanding covering strategic cooperation. A significant aspect of Macron's visit is France's interest in diversifying sources of rare earths, vital for manufacturing magnets used in electric vehicles and electronics. French firm Carester and Malaysian miner Malaco have agreed to collaborate on a rare earths mining and processing project in Perak state. French Ambassador Marc Abensour highlighted readiness to deepen defence cooperation, including countering cyberattacks and disinformation, while France offers support for Malaysia's energy transition and semiconductor industry development.

Japan scrambled fighter jets after two suspected Russian aircraft entered its airspace near Okinoshima shortly after 4:00 a.m. local time on Tuesday. The planes breached Japan's airspace for around three minutes each near Okinoshima in the southwestern area of Fukuoka. Japan lodged a very stern protest with Moscow through diplomatic channels and strongly demanded measures to prevent any recurrence of the airspace violation. The suspected Russian aircraft travelled through the Tsushima Strait and went between Yonaguni island and Taiwan before heading to the Pacific. Relations between Japan and Russia have further soured recently following the arrest of a Japanese journalist on spying allegations and the seizure of assets belonging to a Japanese tobacco firm. On the same day, South Korea also deployed fighter jets and conducted tactical manoeuvres after several Russian military aircraft entered its air defence zone.

David Jones collapsed into administration earlier this month, owing over $400 million to various creditors. The retailer faced accelerating financial losses, including a net loss of $228 million in 2025-26 and a further $69 million loss in the first four months of the new financial year. Outstanding employee entitlements amounted to about $11 million. The collapse followed failed negotiations with its main landlord, Scentre Group, owner of Westfield. Private equity owner Anchorage Capital sought to reduce store numbers to eliminate underperforming suburban locations. Administrators McGrathNicol have called for non-binding indicative offers for the retailer by the end of the month.

Global temperatures have risen above 1.5 degrees Celsius compared with preindustrial levels, surpassing this threshold for several months this year. This marks a significant milestone in climate change, as the 2015 Paris climate agreement aimed to limit warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius. The climate movement has long used the slogan “Keep 1.5 Alive” to emphasise the importance of this target. Scientists warn that a super El Niño year is currently unfolding, which could exacerbate temperature increases and make next year even warmer.

JPMorgan Chase has confirmed a software bug in its internal hardware systems caused delays in transaction processing, affecting thousands of customers across the United States. The issue disrupted transactions involving other banks, including Wells Fargo and Bank of America, and caused delays with Zelle payments. Joe Evangelisti, a spokesperson for JPMorgan Chase, stated the problem was not cyber-related. Reports of service problems peaked around 7pm UK time, according to Downdetector. The bank assured customers they still had access to their accounts and funds, and said transactions are now processing normally following the disruption.

JD Vance has stated that he and former President Donald Trump are completely aligned on the war in Iran. The conflict began on 28 February 2026, launched by the US and Israel, with Trump and senior officials initially claiming it would conclude within weeks. Vance acknowledged the war's political cost to Republicans, a point Trump agreed with, adding that Vance should have said he would undertake the action again. Vance emphasised there is no difference in opinion between himself, Trump, or other senior administration members regarding the Iran war.

Public sector workers in France held a strike and protests on November 17, 2026, to demand wage increases amid inflation. The strike and protests took place across France with about 200 actions planned nationwide. The main protest march in Paris was scheduled to start at 14:00 from Place de la Nation to Place d'Italie. The inter-union coalition calling the strike included CGT, Unsa, FSU, Solidaires, CFE-CGC, the Fédération autonome de la fonction publique, Force ouvrière, and the CFDT public sector branch. Transport disruptions were expected in several regions including Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, Bretagne, Centre-Val de Loire, Hauts-de-France, Nouvelle-Aquitaine, and Occitanie due to the strike. The SNCF reported that high-speed trains were not expected to be affected by the strike. Several metro and RER stations in Paris were closed from 11:00 on November 17 due to the protests. In education, the Snes-FSU and SUD Education unions called for a strike among primary and secondary school staff, with an anticipated 3.73% strike rate in primary public schools. The CGT Federation of ports and docks called for a strike in maritime transport for wages, pensions, and public services.
