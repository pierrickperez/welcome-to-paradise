He discussed with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu the possibility of resuming strikes on Iran. Meanwhile, Iran has warned President Trump that he would not be able to dictate the timing or conditions of a ceasefire if new hostilities occur. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung has announced the country's largest ever budget, with total spending for 2027 set to rise by 12.8% to 820.9 trillion won, equivalent to around $610 billion. A significant portion of this budget, 21.3 trillion won, will be allocated to semiconductors, physical AI, and AI data centres, nearly doubling the investment from 2026. Additionally, 4.7 trillion won will be dedicated to developing a sovereign AI model, which President Lee described as aiming for the world's highest performance. In his annual budget address to parliament, Lee emphasised the commitment to invest boldly in artificial intelligence and future growth sectors. The overall investment in AI and semiconductors is expected to total nearly $16 billion, reflecting the administration's focus on advancing technology and innovation as key drivers of economic growth.

Binance has reported net outflows of $3.64 billion after reopening withdrawals on Sunday, following a $6.3 billion hack. Between 6 a.m. and 3:30 p.m. UTC on November 1, the exchange processed $4.65 billion in withdrawals and $1.01 billion in deposits. These net outflows represent 2.1% of Binance's total assets, which stood at $171 billion as of October 28. Binance resumed withdrawals in phases starting at 6 a.m. UTC, with all 15 networks and assets included in the initial phase restored by 12:36 p.m. The FBI has attributed the hack to North Korean hackers, naming the operation TraderTraitor. Binance has stated it will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds and has covered around 95% of the affected amount, ensuring users will not bear the losses.

Kimi Antonelli has become Formula One's youngest world champion at 20 years and 68 days old, securing the title with four races remaining after finishing third for Mercedes at the Mexico City Grand Prix. Antonelli is the first Italian to win the championship since 1953. The race, held at the high-altitude Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, was won by Max Verstappen, marking his second victory of the season. Antonelli finished behind Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, extending his overall lead to an unassailable 119 points. The previous record for youngest champion was held by Germany's Sebastian Vettel, who won his first title with Red Bull at 23 years and 134 days old in 2010.

Trump also campaigned in Florida over the weekend. Meanwhile, Haley Stevens, a Democratic congresswoman who lost the Senate primary to El-Sayed, has endorsed Republican Mike Rogers. A business jet carrying nine people crashed in South Florida, resulting in the deaths of everyone on board. The aircraft had departed from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was en route to the Bahamas. The crash occurred on November 2, 2026, at approximately 12:50 p.m. Authorities reported that no one on the ground was injured or killed as a result of the incident. However, around 600 residents from a nearby mobile home community were evacuated following the crash for safety reasons.

More than 2.3 million UK households are expected to face a sharp rise in mortgage payments as fixed-rate deals end in 2027, according to data from UK Finance. Thousands of borrowers will come off fixed contracts in the final months of this year, with 700,000 forecast to switch to new rates between July and December. The Bank of England's monetary policy committee is widely expected to raise the base rate from 3.75% to 4% on 5 November, potentially the first of four quarter-point increases over the next year. Mortgage approvals fell to a near three-year low in September, reflecting the impact of rising borrowing costs. Meanwhile, the Office for Budget Responsibility has warned that household disposable incomes will fall by 0.6% next year, and Ofgem forecasts an energy price cap rise of over £400 in January.

The planet is nearing the critical 1.5°C warming threshold, according to a recent UN report. The report warns that ice melt will have large-scale and often irreversible impacts. Glaciers in Greenland and Antarctica are melting six times faster than in the 1990s. Current estimates indicate the world is on a warming trajectory of 2.6°C by the end of the century. However, the UN states that massively reducing greenhouse gas emissions could allow temperatures to fall below the 1.5°C threshold within decades. To restore polar ice caps and ice sheets, warming must be reduced below 1°C.

The world’s largest early warning system for detecting when sick children are deteriorating is being introduced in A&E departments across England. The national paediatric early warning system, known as Pews, uses a scoring system based on vital signs to assess if a child requires urgent attention. This winter marks the first time Pews is being used in A&E, making it the largest standardised scoring system globally. More than six million children attend A&E annually in England. Pews measures vital signs like blood pressure, heart rate, breathing, oxygen levels, and consciousness, with age-appropriate charts designed for rapid decision-making. Over 90 hospitals already use Pews for inpatients, showing consistently lower cardiac arrest rates.

Needles or other sharp metal objects have been found in sweets collected by children while trick-or-treating for Halloween in Germany. Incidents were reported across several regions, including Thuringia, Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Lower Saxony, and Rhineland-Palatinate. In Nuremberg, a child bit down on a needle hidden in a piece of chocolate but was unharmed. In Baden-Württemberg, needles were concealed inside sweets, making them invisible from the outside. Police have launched investigations into attempted aggravated assault in multiple cases, but no suspects have yet been identified in connection with the needle-in-candy incidents.

Russia is nearing completion of a giant radar complex in Kaliningrad capable of monitoring much of Europe. The radar system would reportedly allow Russia to intercept satellite communications and detect aircraft movements and ballistic missiles. The radar's goal is observation of long-distance targets and early warning capabilities, though operational capability is not yet confirmed. Kaliningrad is being developed into a hub for advanced military sensors and electronic warfare systems. Russia has alerted NATO it would be ready to resort to nuclear weapons if the Western alliance attempted to blockade Kaliningrad. Kaliningrad is approximately 200 kilometers from west to east and 100 kilometers from north to south and reportedly can deploy up to 100 nuclear warheads.
