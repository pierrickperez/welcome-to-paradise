Good morning. It's Friday, 13 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Microsoft experienced a widespread outage starting shortly before 6 p.m. British Summer Time on Thursday, continuing into Friday, affecting users globally. Services impacted included Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and Copilot, among other Microsoft 365 offerings. The outage affected Microsoft products in 74 countries and territories, spanning the Americas, parts of Europe, Asia, and Africa, according to Downdetector. Microsoft acknowledged that users may have been unable to connect to multiple Microsoft 365 services and did not provide a timeline for restoring full service. Initially, the company attributed the issue to elevated load across multiple regions, later clarifying that the problem stemmed from several servers being unexpectedly unavailable, which strained the remaining infrastructure and caused some servers to fail automated health checks. Microsoft executive Charles Lamanna confirmed that the outage was not due to a cybersecurity breach. The disruption has been described as a disaster for the Australian business sector, with financial losses estimated in the billions of dollars.

EU leaders met in Dublin on Friday for their first discussion on artificial intelligence, marking a new focus at the summit. However, the meeting was overshadowed by a dispute over who should represent Europe in peace talks concerning Ukraine. European Council President António Costa introduced the AI topic at the informal summit. Meanwhile, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed concerns in a letter to Costa, warning that multiple EU formats in Ukraine negotiations could undermine the EU's credibility and called for a clear mandate. Luxembourg’s Prime Minister Luc Frieden suggested that Costa would be the natural choice as the EU's common representative in the talks. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy urged European leaders to decide on their representative by the end of the month. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz defended the existing E3 format, involving Germany, France, and the UK, as a proven approach for security guarantees in peace negotiations with Russia.

The US Federal Bureau of Investigation has seized cloud infrastructure used by North Korean hackers involved in a video-call scam called FaceStealer, which has stolen over $400 million in cryptocurrency over the past 18 months. The scam involves hackers sending meeting invitations from accounts previously compromised, then playing prerecorded clips of earlier victims to impersonate trusted contacts. The FBI reports that the hackers are now using AI-generated deepfakes of well-known cryptocurrency figures. More than 900 victims, primarily working in crypto and internet-related fields, have fallen prey to the scheme, with an increasing focus on open-source software developers. Under a federal warrant, the FBI has seized over 900 webcam videos of victims. The agency links FaceStealer to a hacking group known as Sapphire Sleet or UNC1069.

The European Union has reached a migration agreement with Nigeria in Abuja, aiming to establish rapid and effective procedures for returning Nigerians residing illegally in EU countries. Under the deal, Nigeria has agreed to readmit its nationals who have no right to stay in Europe. Nigerian media report that EU officials have pledged nearly €100 million in additional funding to support migration management and security cooperation. The EU Commission emphasises that the agreement is not a transactional exchange of concessions. This deal builds on the EU's existing commitment of €731 million in grant funding to Nigeria for the period 2021 to 2027. However, the agreement still requires approval from EU member states and the European Parliament before it can be implemented.

French public sector unions met with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu at Matignon on the morning of November 13, 2026, to demand better salaries. The meeting began at 9:30 AM and was seen as a last chance following unsuccessful talks at Bercy earlier in the week. Unions are calling for salary increases because the recent minimum wage rise does not apply to public sector workers. They also oppose the government's decision to freeze the value of the public sector pay index point for the fourth consecutive year. A public sector strike is planned for November 17, 2026, involving all unions except CFDT. In a letter to the unions, the Prime Minister stated the meeting aims to prioritise concrete measures to improve conditions for public agents, especially those on the lowest incomes.

Generali has reported a strong financial performance in the first nine months of the year, with gross premiums rising to 76.5 billion euros, an increase of 4.5%. The operating result saw a significant rise to 6.446 billion euros, up 8.5%, while the normalized net profit grew by 12% to 3.678 billion euros. The net result also increased by 11.9%, reaching 3.597 billion euros. Additionally, net life insurance inflows remained robust at 11.7 billion euros, marking a 13.6% rise. Cristiano Borean, Generali's Chief Financial Officer, expressed "great confidence in the possibility of exceeding all the objectives of our strategic plan." These figures highlight the company's solid growth and positive outlook for the remainder of the year.

The Greens have decided not to direct preferences to Labor in 15 electorates across Victoria in response to Labor running an open ticket in the inner Melbourne seat of Prahran. The Greens will only recommend voters place the Greens first and One Nation last in these electorates, which include Albert Park, Ashwood, Bellarine, Bendigo East, Bendigo West, Essendon, Euroa, Gippsland East, Mildura, Mornington, Point Cook, Polwarth, South Barwon, Tarneit, and Werribee. Labor had hoped to rely on Greens preferences to regain Prahran, currently held by the Liberals with a slim 1.3 per cent margin after the Greens lost it in a 2025 byelection. Labor is concerned this move could impact their chances in several key seats ahead of the Victorian election on 28 November 2026.

England secured a 2-1 victory over Croatia at Wembley, booking their place in the Nations League quarterfinals. Harry Kane celebrated his record-breaking 126th England cap by scoring a penalty, marking his 92nd goal for the national team. This achievement moves Kane ahead of former goalkeeper Peter Shilton in appearances. The match also featured a pre-game tribute to Kane, with a giant banner displaying his image and the words "England’s Finest." Shilton and Football Association patron Prince William took part in the presentation honouring the captain. Goals from Morgan Rogers and Kane helped England maintain second place in Group A3 behind Spain, confirming their progression to the last eight in March.

Cerebras Systems reported better-than-expected earnings and raised its quarterly guidance, causing its stock to surge 17% in after-hours trading. The company posted adjusted earnings per share of 12 cents, beating the anticipated loss of 14 cents, with core revenue reaching $230.9 million, above the expected $214.7 million. Executives announced a new partnership with Microsoft, signed in October, with revenue from the deal expected to begin in the first quarter of 2027. Cerebras forecasts a core gross margin of 47% to 49% and revenue between $310 million and $330 million for the fourth quarter, anticipating revenue to triple to approximately $2.85 billion in 2027.
