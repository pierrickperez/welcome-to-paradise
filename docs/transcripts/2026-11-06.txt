A US federal judge has temporarily blocked the construction of former President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot memorial arch in Washington for two weeks. The temporary restraining order was issued by US District Judge Tanya Chutkan on Friday. The National Capital Planning Commission had approved the plans for the arch in an 8-4 vote after a nine-hour hearing. The proposed arch would stand 250 feet tall, making it taller than the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, which is about 50 metres high. The project faces opposition from politicians, veterans, and architects who argue that the arch would overshadow the view between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery. The restraining order follows lawsuits filed by the District of Columbia, Virginia, Vietnam veterans, and an architectural historian.

The Democratic Party has gained control of the US Congress following the 2026 midterm elections, flipping eight additional House seats to strengthen their majority. This shift is expected to lead to more aggressive investigations into executive agencies, including the Justice Department. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has expressed hope that the Democrats' victory will result in increased military and financial support for Ukraine. However, former President Donald Trump has pledged to use his veto power extensively, which may limit the Democrats' ability to pass new sanctions against Russia. The newly elected Democrats will officially take office on January 3, 2027.

Paraguay’s rightwing president, Santiago Peña, resigned on Thursday following a political and financial crisis that led to the collapse of the country’s banking system. The crisis intensified last Monday when the central bank ordered Ueno Bank into resolution due to insolvency, freezing deposits of three million customers and leaving many families without access to funds for essentials. Ueno Bank, which rapidly grew under Peña’s leadership from an obscure digital bank to one of Paraguay’s largest lenders, reportedly served about 60% of the adult population. Peña, in office since August 2023 with a term until 2028, faced impeachment proceedings supported by lawmakers who unanimously voted to launch them over allegations of misconduct and conflicts of interest linked to his previous shareholding in Ueno Holding. The bank managed between $800 million and $850 million in deposits from state and pension funds, raising concerns about the intertwining of public money and private business. Opposition senator Rafael Filizzola described the case as "the biggest fraud carried out from political power in the democratic era."

Singapore Airlines is rolling out new onboard seating, dining, and entertainment options as part of an over S$2 billion ($1.6 billion) investment. New premium cabin seats for long-haul flights will progressively debut on the Airbus A350 long-haul and ultra-long-range planes in mid-2027. First-class seats will come with 32-inch 4K high-definition screens, Bang and Olufsen noise-cancelling headphones, and options for passengers to book double bed and face-to-face dining configurations. Seating in business class will come outfitted with 22-inch 4K screens, wireless charging, and Bluetooth audio connectivity. The new products will also debut with the Boeing 777-9 aircraft when they come into service with Singapore Airlines. Singapore Airlines faces a long-running delay to the delivery of new Boeing 777-9 planes, originally slated for 2021, with an uncertain delivery date. Minor refreshes will be added to premium economy and economy classes, including an extra inch of reclining space and new upholstery, with new 4K seatback entertainment monitors on incoming 777-9 aircraft. The announcement was made on November 6, 2026, following a media briefing on November 4 by SIA Chief Commercial Officer Lee Lik Hsin.

The French national anti-terror prosecutor has reported a rise in investigations related to violent radical far-right groups. Olivier Christen, speaking on RTL on November 3, 2026, confirmed that the number of procedures linked to these groups is increasing. This follows a violent radical far-right attack on October 23, 2026, in Trappes and La Verrière, which resulted in the death of a 26-year-old black Frenchman and left seven others injured, four seriously. Since its creation, the national anti-terror prosecutor's office has judged 14 procedures related to violent radical far-right groups, including one before a special juvenile court. While jihadist terrorism remains the main terrorism threat in France, the violent radical far-right threat is on the rise.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has called on Latin American leaders to urgently assist in repairing Ukraine's power grid, which has been extensively damaged by Russian attacks. Speaking at the 30th Ibero-American Summit in Madrid, Zelenskyy highlighted that every power plant in Ukraine has suffered damage. He requested technical equipment including transformers, power cables, electronic devices, and diesel and gas generators to maintain the country's infrastructure. Zelenskyy urged leaders to support Ukraine by facilitating connections with their companies to expedite the delivery of these supplies. The summit, which brought together 22 mainly Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking Latin American nations, also heard Zelenskyy's appeal for leaders to condemn what he described as Russian "mass killings" carried out through bombings and drones.

An energy minister has told the bosses of Great Britain’s biggest energy suppliers to do more to help households get on top of their unpaid energy bills this winter. Energy bosses were summoned to a roundtable meeting on Thursday to discuss the government’s plan to pay down the energy debts of about 2.4 million of the hardest-hit households. The government plans to spend almost £2.5 billion to help write off the country’s record high energy debts. The minister for energy consumers, Polly Billington, told energy company bosses that suppliers would still need to play a “crucial role” in tackling problem debt alongside the government’s support package. Suppliers are expected to proactively identify customers under financial strain and offer help such as debt payment plans or debt advice services. The government is backing the use of smart meters and smart pre-pay meters to help households track spending and avoid arrears. The meeting was attended by senior leaders from the largest six energy suppliers, Energy UK, National Energy Action, and five consumer advice groups including Citizens Advice.

India's stock market has attracted renewed interest from foreign investors, who have shifted from net sellers to net buyers in recent days, investing over $2.3 billion according to NSDL data. Rajiv Batra told CNBC that India has emerged as an 'AI hedge' for portfolios looking to diversify from crowded AI trades. Despite a broader sell-off on Wall Street, India's benchmark indexes traded higher, supported by earnings growth of more than 20% among listed companies. Consensus earnings per share estimates have also been revised up slightly for 2026 and 2027. India currently trades at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 18.6 times, higher than China and Brazil. Batra noted that if foreign investors neutralize their underweight positions, net inflows could reach around $115 billion.
