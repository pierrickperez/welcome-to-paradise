Good morning. It's Tuesday, 3 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

The U.S. military conducted limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday, targeting surface-to-air missiles reportedly used against U.S. assets. Iranian state media confirmed that five explosions were heard on the island. These strikes were described by U.S. officials as acts of self-defense in response to Iran's ongoing efforts to target maritime shipping and American assets. The timing of the strikes is notable, occurring on the eve of the U.S. midterm elections. This development contrasts with earlier statements from President Donald Trump, who had said the U.S. would not launch strikes on Iran before Tuesday's elections. The situation remains fluid as both sides monitor the impact of these actions.

US voters are heading to the polls today for the midterm elections, with all 435 House of Representatives seats and 35 Senate seats up for grabs. Democrats are widely projected to retake control of the House, needing to gain three seats to do so. However, control of the Senate remains uncertain, as Senate Democrats require a net gain of four seats to secure a majority. More than 230 million eligible voters across the country can cast their ballots. Polling stations open earliest in Vermont at 5am local time, with voting closing at various times across states, including 8pm in Michigan and Iowa, and 7pm in Kansas. In Michigan, a notable development has occurred with Representative Haley Stevens endorsing Republican Mike Rogers over the Democratic candidate Abdul Al-Sayed in the Senate race.

North Korea has denied involvement in the $6.3 billion Binance hack, describing the accusation as a 'grave political provocation.' Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, criticised the FBI's attribution of the attack to the North Korea-linked hacking group TraderTraitor. The FBI, alongside blockchain analytics firms Elliptic and Chainalysis, has linked the Binance hack and stolen funds to actors connected to North Korea. Binance has responded by progressively reopening withdrawals for additional crypto assets across more networks following the incident. The company has covered approximately 95% of the affected amount, with the remaining 5% consisting of less liquid tokens. Kim Yo Jong accused the US of misleading international public opinion by portraying cybercrimes worldwide as the work of North Korea, calling the accusations 'absurd rhetoric' and a 'sinister attempt' to damage Pyongyang's public image.

The Reserve Bank of Australia has raised its benchmark cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85%, the highest level since November 2008. This decision, announced at the end of a two-day monetary policy meeting, marks the fifth rate increase this year. RBA governor Michele Bullock acknowledged the move was "not the news that many Australians want to hear," but said it was necessary to prevent "even worse pain down the track." The decision was a split one, with six board members voting for the increase and three opting to hold. Economists polled by Reuters had expected the rise. Following the announcement, the Australian stock market ended higher, reflecting investor confidence in the central bank's action.

Japan is considering a defence spending plan of nearly 500 billion dollars over five years, which would almost double current defence investments and enhance its military capabilities. The new funds are expected to finance purchases such as large quantities of drones. In the fiscal year 2025, Japan bought US weapons through the Foreign Military Sales programme for about 993 billion yen, an increase of approximately 110 billion yen from the previous year. Major acquisitions included stealth fighters F-35A and F-35B, costing 222.7 billion yen, and KC-46A refuelling aircraft for 222.4 billion yen. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi aims to strengthen the Tokyo-Washington alliance amid rising regional tensions.

Around 2,800 troops from 10 European countries will take part in the first exercise of the Multinational Force - Ukraine in southeast Poland later this month. The exercise, named Koalicja, will run from 9 to 22 November near the Ukrainian border. The largest group of soldiers, 1,100, will come from France, with the UK contributing 300 troops. Other participating countries include Belgium, Estonia, Germany, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Poland, Spain and Sweden. Only light equipment such as drones will be used for anti-drone and self-defence drills, with no heavy weaponry or tanks deployed. Established in 2025, the MNF-U has its operational headquarters in Paris. The exercise is not a NATO operation and involves minimal US participation, aiming to test rapid deployment capabilities to support Ukraine post-conflict.

Garment workers in Bangladesh have continued their protests for a third day, demanding a minimum wage increase to 30,000 taka, more than double the current 12,500 taka. On Sunday, police used tear gas and stun grenades to disperse demonstrators in the Jamgora area near Dhaka, arresting dozens. The Bangladesh Garment Manufacturers and Exporters Association reported disruptions at 529 factories, affecting nearly a million workers. The government has formed a six-member wage board to review the demands. Approximately four million garment workers, half of them women, are involved in the protests. The last minimum wage revision was in December 2023, but inflation has continued to rise. Worker Laili Begum earns around 15,000 taka monthly, increasing with overtime, yet struggles to meet basic needs.

The United Kingdom has revised its immigration rules, doubling the time most foreign workers must wait to qualify for permanent residency to 10 years. Previously, permanent residency was granted after five years. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said hundreds of thousands of care workers will be exempt from the hardline rules, able to settle after five years if they meet visa conditions and earn at least £25,000 a year. People earning over £50,000 can still apply after five years, and those earning more than £125,000 after three years. Refugees arriving through new safe and legal routes can settle after five years, while others face waits of up to 20 years, reduced to 10 if they work or study. Illegal arrivals may wait up to 30 years. The new rules apply retroactively to workers already in the country, with an estimated 1.4 million people expected to receive settled status between 2026 and 2030.

The Danish government is seeking a national ban on the Ross 308 breed, known as the turbo chicken, and aims to extend this prohibition across Europe. This breed, common in Danish poultry production, grows from about 50 grams to 2 kilograms in just 33 days. Christian Rabjerg Madsen, Denmark’s minister for nature and animal welfare, discourages Danes from buying turbo chickens and supports a ban at both national and EU levels. The proposal has cross-party support in Denmark. Industry experts stress that any ban must include imported chickens to improve animal welfare. Britta Riis of Animal Protection Denmark describes turbo chickens as a "deeply unethical and grotesque form of breeding" causing systematic suffering.

Jodie Ginsberg, Chief Executive Officer of the Committee to Protect Journalists, has highlighted the rising global death toll among media workers. She emphasises that the killing of journalists is not only an attack on those reporting the news but also undermines the public's right to know, institutional accountability, and democratic foundations. Ginsberg warns of a growing normalization of harassment, intimidation, and violence against journalists, alongside a persistent failure to hold perpetrators accountable. She stresses that allowing killers of journalists to go unpunished creates conditions for further attacks.
