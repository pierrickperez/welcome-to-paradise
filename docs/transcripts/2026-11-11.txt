Good morning. It's Wednesday, 11 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Peace talks between Ukraine and Russia, mediated by the United States, began in Abu Dhabi at the Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental hotel on November 10, 2026. These discussions mark the first meeting between the two sides since February 2026, following a pause while Washington concentrated on the conflict in Iran. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky expressed satisfaction with the change in approach by Washington during the talks. On November 11, the focus was on de-escalation measures concerning energy and the Black Sea. President Zelensky emphasised the necessity of security guarantees for Ukraine throughout the negotiations. The United States aims to broaden the negotiation format to include European representatives, with Zelensky stating that Europe should designate its representative during November 2026.

The House of Representatives effectively ended an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth with a procedural vote on Tuesday evening. By a margin of 211 to 204, the House approved a measure to refer the impeachment resolution to a committee, effectively halting the effort for now. Republican congressman Thomas Massie of Kentucky initiated the impeachment effort and was the only Republican to vote against moving the resolution to committee. President Donald Trump praised the outcome, calling the impeachment effort a failure and describing Hegseth as a "Great American Patriot." Hegseth condemned the impeachment attempt as a "farce" and "sabotage," warning it endangered national security. Democrats are set to take control of Congress in January, which may influence future proceedings.

Germany has fallen to 25th place in the Climate Change Performance Index, marking its third consecutive year of decline and its worst ranking since 2019. The country received a "medium" overall rating, being outperformed by Brazil, Colombia, and Romania. Germany's poor showing in national climate policy is linked to current strategies pursued by Economy Minister Katherina Reiche. A European Commission report from November 9 highlighted that Germany is among nine EU nations projected to miss their 2030 emissions reduction targets. Meanwhile, solar energy accounted for 75% of global electricity demand growth last year, according to the energy think tank Ember. The top three positions on the index remain unfilled, as no country has yet met global climate goals. The G20 countries, responsible for about 80% of global CO2 emissions, continue to perform poorly in climate action rankings.

Alibaba has announced that the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will permit the company to spin off its semiconductor subsidiary, T-Head. The company plans to offer shares of T-Head to the public in Hong Kong, pending approval from both the exchange and the China Securities Regulatory Commission. Alibaba had already filed confidentially on September 11 to list T-Head on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. However, the filing did not specify a date or the proposed size of the listing. This move marks a significant step for Alibaba as it seeks to expand its presence in the semiconductor industry through a public offering in Hong Kong.

Markets are in a "state of alert," according to analysts monitoring a pivot from riskier tech sector bets into defensive stocks. Wall Street's tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite Index is up by around 8.7% since the start of the year as of Nov. 9 close, but gave up some of its year-to-date gains in October when it shed 5.3% over the course of the month. South Korea's Kospi index, which is heavily exposed to the semiconductor sector, lost 16.2% in October and is down more than 8% month-to-date. The MSCI World Information Technology index lost 7% in October, and has shed a further 4.6% so far this month. MSCI's indexes encompassing the consumer staples, healthcare and utilities sectors have all risen significantly over the last month. AJ Bell's analysis shows healthcare and consumer staples stocks have been the best performers after energy in the second half of 2026 so far. The IT sector has gone from being the best-performing sector in the first half of the year on a 28.4% rise, to the worst performer in the second half after tumbling more than 11%.

A bus collided with 32 vehicles, including passenger cars, a truck, and four other buses in Seongnam, South Korea, on Wednesday morning, injuring 57 people. The fire department of Gyeonggi Province reported that nine of the injured are in serious condition, though none are life-threatening. The crash occurred during rush hour traffic in Seongnam, just south of Seoul. The bus driver, believed to be in his 60s, told investigators that the brakes failed. He tested negative in a breathalyser test and was hospitalised after being freed from the vehicle. Dozens of fire personnel and vehicles responded to the scene, where an emergency medical station was also established.

Corentin Horeau has won the Route du Rhum in the Imoca category, finishing the race after 9 days, 17 hours, 12 minutes and 9 seconds at sea. He crossed the finish line in Guadeloupe at 6:14 AM Paris time. In the Ocean Fifty category, Basile Bourgnon claimed victory, crossing the finish line just two minutes before Horeau at 6:12 AM Paris time. Bourgnon's win marks a significant moment, coming 28 years after his father Laurent's last triumph in the race. The Route du Rhum is a solo transatlantic yacht race, attracting skilled sailors competing across different boat classes.

The European Commission has announced new rules to regulate influencer activity online. Influencers will be required to clearly declare if their videos are subject to advertising contracts or commercial cooperation. Terms such as "collab", "contenu sponsorisé", and "ambassadeur" will be banned for being too vague. Additionally, influencers will no longer be allowed to sell products to minors online. These measures are part of the Commission's upcoming "Digital Fairness Act", set to be unveiled on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, aiming to strengthen consumer protection on digital platforms.

Antonia Romeo, the cabinet secretary, has criticised Whitehall as too "tame, reactive and risk-averse" and announced a "completely new approach" to managing civil servant performance. Speaking in a lecture, she said the new system would "incentivise and reward brilliant delivery, valuable expertise and sticking it out to get the job done." Romeo aims to reduce staff churn, where officials move sideways for pay rises rather than being rewarded for staying in the same role. She is overseeing a review of the civil service organisation and code, due in the spring. After the speech, a government spokesperson said Burnham "backs the cabinet secretary’s vision for a civil service that is fit for the future."
