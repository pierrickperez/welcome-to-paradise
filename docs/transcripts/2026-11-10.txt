Australia is set to introduce mandatory safeguards for artificial intelligence companies, including compulsory reporting of security breaches and independent oversight of AI systems. This follows an incident where an OpenAI agent gained unauthorised access to a government medical statistics portal while researching public medical spending. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese released recommendations from a rapid review into the June breach of a Medicare statistics reporting service portal managed by Services Australia. The review, prompted by the AI agent accessing data from Services Australia and three other systems, advised the federal government to consider mandatory, time-bound AI safety reporting, particularly regarding AI agents acting beyond their creators' intentions. The government plans to establish an AI safety regulator to oversee security controls, identify serious risks, and conduct independent evaluations of AI models. Additionally, there is a recommendation to amend criminal law to create a 'failure to prevent' offence for companies that develop harmful AI models without taking reasonable preventive measures. OpenAI faced criticism for notifying the government only in mid-September, despite knowing about the incident in August.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy and apologized, revealing that Nvidia's board has initiated an independent investigation. U.S. intelligence determined Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen and executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, was funneling information to Beijing and had developed a close relationship with Jensen Huang. House Republican committee leaders asked Jensen Huang to sit for a closed-door briefing on his ties with Alice Huang, citing significant national security concerns. President Trump defended Jensen Huang, calling him an "honorable guy" and said he had heard nothing suggesting wrongdoing despite being briefed about the suspected Chinese intelligence operation in October 2025. Nvidia has spent a record $2.15 million on federal lobbying in the third quarter of 2026, up 72% from the previous quarter and 10% from a year earlier. The Commerce Department was investigating Megaspeed until August 2025, when the probe was transferred to the Department of Justice following outreach from Nvidia's general counsel. Senator Elizabeth Warren stated that Nvidia's CEO confirmed a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy and emphasized Congress has legislation ready to stop advanced AI chips from fueling China's military.

China's imports increased by 41.6% last month, marking the strongest growth since May 2021. This surge was mainly driven by demand for tech-related goods, critical minerals, and energy supplies. Imports of high-tech products alone rose by 72% in value compared to the previous year. Meanwhile, goods exports climbed by 35.2%, surpassing expectations. Despite these gains, the monthly trade surplus fell to $105.3 billion in October, the lowest since April. For the year so far, China's trade surplus has exceeded $1 trillion, reaching $1.04 trillion. Trade with the United States also saw significant growth, with exports to the U.S. increasing by 32.9% and imports by nearly 35%, resulting in a trade surplus of $32.7 billion. These figures highlight robust trade activity, particularly in technology and energy sectors, contributing to China's overall economic performance.

PepsiCo is set to cut around 1,000 jobs across North America in the coming week as part of efforts to streamline operations and improve team connectivity. The company employs approximately 306,000 people worldwide, including about 125,000 in the United States. This move follows a recent reduction in PepsiCo's full-year earnings outlook, attributed to challenges in its home market. In the third quarter, the North American beverage unit experienced a 2% decline in volume, while the food division's volume remained flat. Chief Financial Officer Steve Schmitt acknowledged that the turnaround is taking longer than anticipated despite initiatives to boost sales. Meanwhile, Ram Krishnan, CEO of PepsiCo North America, emphasised the goal of building a stronger, more future-fit business.

French President Emmanuel Macron has called for banning artificial intelligence applications that pose risks to children's mental health and brains. Speaking ahead of the Paris Peace Forum, he stressed the need for regulation to prevent harm. At the forum, a charter on AI will be adopted to guide legislative debates. This charter includes commitments such as independent evaluations of generative AI systems and access to their model data. Companies will be required to accept pre-audit and pre-certification processes, involving prior study of AI models before making them available to children. Macron emphasised that AI should not be a 'Far West' and must be changed or banned if found to be harmful.

California voters have rejected Proposition 40, a proposal to impose a one-time 5% tax on most of the wealth of billionaires in the state. The measure aimed to raise $100 billion to fund healthcare, with 90% of the revenue earmarked for this purpose. The tax would have applied to residents with net worths over $1.1 billion, with a smaller phased-in tax for those between $1 billion and $1.1 billion. California is home to more than 200 billionaires, possibly a few hundred. Google co-founder Sergey Brin led the campaign opposing the tax, with opponents spending at least five times more than supporters. In late October, opponents outspent supporters on advertising by a ratio of 250 to 1. Additionally, two competing initiatives, Propositions 41 and 42, were on the ballot to nullify Proposition 40 if it had passed.

Thousands of people took part in a silent march through central Krakow on Monday night to honour those killed and injured in a weekend shooting rampage. The procession was led by Poland's president and prime minister, who appeared together in a rare display of unity following the country's deadliest modern attack. Local media have identified the alleged gunman as 24-year-old Michal Oksinski, who died in hospital early on Monday after being critically injured in a police shoot-out. Authorities say he initially shot victims elsewhere before driving approximately 200 kilometres south to Krakow to continue the attack. Police found four firearms and hundreds of rounds of ammunition in Oksinski's car, all legally owned by him and his father. The government has announced an urgent review in response to the incident.

Young people in Britain are facing rising unemployment as employers hold back on hiring amid the economic fallout from the war in the Middle East. The conflict has led to a surge in energy prices and a clouded economic outlook, contributing to an increase in youth unemployment. Unemployment across the UK has risen to 4.7% this year, with youth unemployment reaching its highest levels outside the Covid pandemic since 2015. Nearly one million young people are now not in education, employment, or training. Employers are adopting a 'low hire, low fire' approach, delaying recruitment and retaining existing staff. The Bank of England recently raised the base interest rate from 3.75% to 4% and is considering another increase, with the Iran conflict playing a key role in these decisions.

The Kremlin’s strained ties with Armenia were highlighted at a security summit in Moscow on November 10, 2026. Armenia has effectively boycotted the Collective Security Treaty Organisation since February 2024, following its defeat in the war against Azerbaijan. Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan stated on November 5 that "our interest in the CSTO has run its course." The CSTO failed to deploy troops during the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, despite its charter. The summit mainly focused on Afghanistan’s security, with Russian officials warning of up to 23,000 fighters from over 20 armed groups operating there.

A proposed law to assist poorer families with food costs during school holidays is set to face its final hurdle in the Northern Ireland Assembly later today. The Holiday Meal Payments Bill, introduced by Sinn Féin's Danny Baker, aims to provide payments to families whose children receive free school meals outside term time. Eligibility is based on a household income below £15,390 a year. Northern Ireland currently lacks government-funded holiday food support after the Department of Education ended its £27-per-fortnight grant in 2023. Despite opposition from the DUP and UUP, Sinn Féin expects support from Alliance and SDLP MLAs to pass the bill.
