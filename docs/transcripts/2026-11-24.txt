Jensen Huang has returned as CEO of Nvidia after resigning less than two weeks ago. His comeback has sparked renewed scrutiny of the company's corporate governance and its dependence on its founder. On the day of Huang's return, Nvidia shares surged nearly 32%, boosting the company's market value by close to $1 trillion in a single session. Former US President Donald Trump publicly supported Huang's return, describing him as "a Great Patriot and National Treasure." Huang is scheduled to face a public Senate hearing on US export controls on 3 December 2026. The recent leadership changes have highlighted vulnerabilities in Nvidia's board oversight and succession planning. Analysts have suggested that the company needs to strengthen its corporate governance, improve succession planning, and appoint more experienced board members to ensure stability and long-term growth.

A growing number of Germans now oppose providing military aid to Ukraine, according to recent survey data. The majority of respondents believe Germany should maintain a neutral stance between the US and China. Nearly half, 48%, support a more restrained approach to international affairs, marking a five percentage point increase from last year. Concerns about global stability are high, with 65% fearing further military conflicts worldwide. Additionally, 77% of those surveyed want Germany to prioritise diplomacy over military action. Support for greater international engagement has declined, with only 40% in favour, down from 48% in 2025. Regional differences are notable, as support for neutrality stands at 92% in eastern Germany compared to 81% in the west. These figures suggest a significant shift in public opinion towards caution and diplomatic solutions in Germany's foreign policy.

Bangladesh's former prime minister Sheikh Hasina has announced plans to return home on 16 December, more than two years after a popular uprising forced her from power. Hasina, who has lived in exile in Delhi since August 2024, has requested the Bangladeshi government to arrange her flight back to Dhaka, according to her spokesperson. Last year, a Bangladeshi court sentenced her to death in absentia for crimes against humanity related to a deadly crackdown on student-led protests that ended her 15-year rule, charges she denies. The state minister for foreign affairs, Humayun Kabir, dismissed the possibility of her returning on her own terms, stating she will be brought back in handcuffs. Hasina fled to India on 5 August 2024 amid mass protests and after crowds stormed her official residence. The UN estimates that up to 1,400 people were killed during the unrest. Her spokesperson accused Bangladeshi authorities of forcing her into exile in violation of the constitution. The chosen return date coincides with Bangladesh's Victory Day, marking the end of its 1971 war of independence.

Sébastien Lecornu has sent a letter to parliamentarians proposing five themes of compromise on the 2027 budget. This move comes as the Prime Minister aims to reach a budget agreement without exceeding the government's deficit target of 5%. Earlier in November, the budget project was unanimously rejected by deputies and is now under examination by the Senate. The proposals seek to address concerns raised during the initial rejection and facilitate a consensus on the budget moving forward.

Only 1,283 asylum seekers will be relocated between EU countries in 2026, the first year of the bloc's new migration rules. This number is a small fraction of the 669,000 people who applied for asylum in the EU in 2025. The Pact on Migration and Asylum's principle of "mandatory solidarity" has met resistance from most member states, which have repeatedly watered down the relocation mechanism. While the European Commission initially proposed relocating 30,000 asylum seekers from Italy, Spain, Greece, and Cyprus, only 562 have been transferred so far, all from Cyprus to Lithuania, Romania, Bulgaria, and France. A diplomat said further flights from Cyprus are scheduled before the end of the year, bringing the total to 1,283. In December 2025, EU interior ministers agreed on 21,000 relocations "or other solidarity efforts" and €420 million in financial contributions, scaling back the Commission's original proposal. Hungary and Slovakia refused to contribute anything, in an apparent breach of the mandatory solidarity principle that the Commission has yet to sanction. Cyprus is therefore the only frontline country from which asylum seekers have actually been relocated under the scheme.

Meta is launching its Muse AI assistant in the UK, encouraging users to try it on their apps and in stores. Muse has been popular in the US, ranking first and second on Google and Apple’s free app stores, with millions of downloads. The assistant can be used as a standalone app or within Meta’s WhatsApp messaging platform. Meta has partnered with British businesses including Marks & Spencer, Currys, Sainsbury’s and easyJet to help Muse navigate their websites. Reuters recently reported concerns from Meta employees about Muse’s performance, such as incorrect message reminders and random bank account balances appearing in messages. Meta said internal testing is crucial for safety and privacy, and most issues reported were due to a bug that has now been fixed.

COP31 concluded on Sunday without reaching a climate agreement after 44 hours of overtime following two weeks of negotiations. The final draft pact, proposed by co-hosts Türkiye and Australia, was rejected by the EU, the UK, and many small Pacific island states because it made no mention of fossil fuels. The draft did not include the 'transition away from fossil fuels' or TAFF, a key agreement from COP28 in Dubai. Additionally, a target of around €110 billion annually for climate adaptation finance by 2035 was removed. Oil-producing nations, led by Saudi Arabia, opposed explicit references to coal, oil, and gas. Over 130 countries demanded the final agreement reaffirm the commitment to transition away from fossil fuels. Ethiopia will host COP32 next year, facing similar unresolved issues.

Businesses across the Asia-Pacific region report that their investments in artificial intelligence are paying off, with the technology helping boost productivity and revenue. A survey of 9,750 business leaders across ten markets—Australia, Indonesia, Japan, Malaysia, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Thailand, and Vietnam—found that 58% of those using AI said their investments had already surpassed initial costs. However, only 12% expressed strong confidence in accurately measuring their return on investment. AI adoption is growing across all surveyed markets, with Australia leading at 61%, followed by Singapore at 59% and South Korea at 58%. Despite this growth, 48% of respondents identified a shortage of AI and digital skills as a significant barrier to further adoption. To address these challenges, the Asian Development Bank and South Korea have established a $20 million fund to support developing economies in adopting AI and improving digital infrastructure.

Draft laws proposed by the Coalition and the Greens aim to protect whistleblowers reporting corruption or misconduct in sports. The government's bill, introduced to parliament in August, is currently in the Senate and expected to be voted on this week. Under the draft legislation, whistleblowers would be protected if they provide information in good faith to law enforcement, a sporting body, or Sport Integrity Australia. The bill also introduces new offences criminalising behaviours like the manipulation of sporting events. Greens leader David Shoebridge has urged Labor to establish a dedicated whistleblower protection scheme for sport. Notably, Australian cricketers Steve Smith, David Warner, and Cameron Bancroft faced significant bans following the ball-tampering scandal.

A new report by Human Rights Watch concludes that Prime Minister Modi's Hindu nationalist government has systematically weakened institutions meant to protect human rights and democratic freedoms. The 95-page report, titled 'So What's This Democracy We're Talking About?: Modi's Authoritarian Playbook in India,' finds that the right-wing Bharatiya Janata Party-led (BJP) government is undermining parliamentary procedures, suppressing opposition, and weaponizing investigative agencies for political ends. The report also claims that the Election Commission of India and the Supreme Court, both independent bodies, failed to uphold fundamental freedoms, citing the elimination of millions of voter names from India's electoral rolls in a controversial exercise. Separately, Saurav Das, one of the co-conveners of India's political pressure group Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), said he was prevented from leaving New Delhi to deliver talks about the movement in the UK after immigration officers told him his passport had been "flagged." In another incident, a man who was called into a police station for counselling over a family dispute allegedly pulled out a knife and went on a stabbing spree, injuring nine police officers in New Delhi.
