Scott Colom has been elected as the first Black senator in Mississippi in nearly 150 years, defeating incumbent Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith. Colom secured 49.2% of the vote, narrowly surpassing Hyde-Smith's 48.7%, with a margin of 4,585 votes. This victory marks Colom as the first Black senator ever directly elected by Mississippi voters and the first to serve since 1881. He is also the first Democratic senator from Mississippi since 1989. The Associated Press projected Colom's win eight days after the election, with 99% of ballots counted. Colom claimed victory in a speech at Tougaloo College in Jackson, Mississippi. His election contributes to the Democrats holding at least 55 of the 100 Senate seats.

An Israeli airstrike has hit a building at Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza, resulting in the deaths of at least five Palestinians and injuring over 20 others. The strike caused significant damage to the hospital's intensive care and surgical departments. The Israeli military confirmed the attack, stating it targeted a senior Hamas member and other militants who were allegedly using the hospital as a command centre for military and terrorist activities. The Gaza Civil Defence agency, which is run by Hamas, reported six fatalities and more than 20 injuries from the strike. Mahmoud Basal, a spokesperson for the Hamas-run Civil Defence, also confirmed six people were killed in the incident. The differing death toll figures reflect the ongoing conflict and the challenges in verifying information from the area.

US mediators Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow on November 12, 2026, to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin. They were welcomed at Vnukovo Airport by Kirill Dmitriev, the Russian President's special representative. Prior to their arrival in Moscow, the envoys spent two days in the United Arab Emirates engaging in trilateral peace talks in Abu Dhabi, which resulted in an agreement on a prisoner exchange between Russia and Ukraine. Following their meeting with President Putin, Kushner and Witkoff are expected to travel to Ireland to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

At least two people have died and seventeen others were injured, including seven children, in the partial collapse of a three-storey building in Brooklyn, New York City. The incident occurred around 4pm local time on Wednesday near a construction excavation site. Four of the injured children are reported to be seriously hurt. Firefighters and emergency responders are conducting a challenging search-and-rescue operation amid dangerous debris and unstable cavities beneath the surface. The building, constructed in 1930 according to city records, partially gave way, complicating rescue efforts. Authorities continue to work at the scene to locate and assist any remaining victims.

Gas customers in Jersey will face a rise in their bills of about 5% from 1 December 2026, Island Energy has announced. The increase will affect both households and businesses. The company said the rise reflects higher wholesale gas and liquefied petroleum gas costs, caused by disruption to exports and shipping routes in the Middle East following the conflict with Iran. Island Energy's chief executive, Graeme Millar, acknowledged that any price increase is unwelcome, especially during winter. He added that securing fuel at lower prices in advance had limited the impact of wholesale cost increases but could not fully protect customers from sustained rises. Island Energy also encouraged customers concerned about paying their gas bills to contact its customer team to discuss available support.

India's cabinet has approved plans to develop the Yuge Yugeen Bharat Museum, which will showcase the journey of India's civilisation. The museum will cover nearly 178,509 square metres and is expected to welcome around 10 million visitors annually. The first gallery, themed around adornment, is set to open to the public next year, with additional galleries to follow gradually. The government has identified approximately 1.8 million objects for potential display, sourced from national and state museums, as well as archaeological and private collections.

Scientists are mapping two remote coral atolls in the Pacific Ocean to support local and global conservation efforts. Bikar and Bokak, which became the Marshall Islands' first national marine sanctuaries in 2025, are being studied to establish a comprehensive ecological baseline across the 48,000-square-kilometre site, from sky to land and sea. The atolls lie around 116 and 385 kilometres from the nearest inhabited atoll. Researchers found some of the most intact ecosystems remaining in the region, with 15 breeding or likely breeding seabird species, over 260 reef fish species, and open-water predators such as sharks, jacks, and wahoo. Nesting green sea turtles were also observed at Bikar. The project aims to understand what has been lost elsewhere and to track future climate-driven changes.

The SS United States has been sunk off the coast of Okaloosa County, Florida, to become the world's largest artificial reef. The ship was deliberately sunk on Wednesday, November 12, 2026, after a sinking process lasting about five hours. Rather than using explosives, plugs in the hull were removed to allow water to fill the vessel naturally. Okaloosa County purchased the SS United States in 2024 for $1 million with the intention of converting it into an artificial reef. The ship now rests approximately 20 nautical miles from the beaches of Destin, Florida. It is expected to become a popular diving destination and provide habitat for marine life such as red snapper, grouper, amberjack, mackerel, and tuna. The SS United States was launched in 1951 and was once the fastest ocean liner in the world, holding a transatlantic speed record.

Alternative investments now make up 60% of single-family office portfolios, surpassing traditional assets for the first time, according to BNY Wealth. Traditional assets accounted for 41% of allocations, the report found. JPMorgan Asset Management's private wealth alternative assets in Asia-Pacific surged 40% over the past year, said Dan Watkins, the firm's regional chief executive officer. Net inflows into open-ended infrastructure funds rose 30% year on year through the first quarter of 2026, according to PitchBook data cited by UBS. This shift reflects investor concerns over concentrated equity markets, geopolitical uncertainty, and doubts about stocks and bonds' protective roles. However, a Citi survey showed public equities recorded the strongest net increase in allocations, indicating wealthy investors are adding alternatives rather than abandoning traditional markets.

The European Political Community summit gathered over 50 leaders in Dublin on November 12, 2026. Despite the large attendance, insiders do not widely regard the summit as useful, with some hoping it will end after French President Emmanuel Macron leaves office. Meanwhile, an informal European Council meeting is also taking place in Dublin on Friday, attracting more enthusiasm from leaders. Key agenda items at the European Council meeting include deciding who should represent Europe in future Ukraine peace talks, as well as discussions on artificial intelligence and cybersecurity.

Bolivian authorities arrested Aldo Quezada, a former senior judge, at his home in Santa Cruz, eastern Bolivia, on suspicion of drug trafficking and criminal association. Quezada resigned last month as chief justice of the Santa Cruz departmental court after being named to a US visa restrictions list for alleged links to drug trafficking. He is among several senior Bolivian judicial officials arrested since September on suspicion of links to drug cartels. These arrests are part of a US-backed crackdown against officials involved in suspected cartel activity in Latin America. Bolivia's conservative President Rodrigo Paz assumed office on November 8, 2025, and has sought closer ties to the US. Paz has signed Bolivia up to US President Donald Trump's "Shield of the Americas," a new US-backed security alliance for the Americas.
