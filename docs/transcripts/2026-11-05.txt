Good morning. It's Thursday, 5 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has expressed hope that the Democratic Party's gains in the recent United States midterm elections will lead to increased support for Kyiv. American voters handed control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives to the Democrats. Zelenskyy noted that Democrats clearly advocate for supporting Ukraine and providing assistance, which he believes could influence Russian President Vladimir Putin. Meanwhile, the European Union’s foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas, has also expressed hope that the new US Congress will continue backing Ukraine and intensify pressure on Russia. In related developments, Steve Witkoff, an envoy for former President Donald Trump, announced plans to bring Ukrainian and Russian delegations together in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11 to discuss ending the war and security arrangements for Ukraine. Zelenskyy revealed he has had detailed conversations with Witkoff and Trump’s son-in-law, Jared Kushner, ahead of the meeting.

New Zealand's general election on November 7, 2026, is anticipated to be closely contested, with neither of the major parties expected to secure an outright majority. The country operates under a mixed-member proportional system, where 120 parliamentary seats require at least 61 for a majority. Incumbent Prime Minister Christopher Luxon, leading the National Party, is seeking a second term, while Labour leader Chris Hipkins aims to return to power after the party's 2023 defeat. Smaller parties are set to play a pivotal role, holding the balance of power and potentially deciding the election outcome. To gain seats, these parties must either surpass the five percent party vote threshold or win an electorate seat. Notably, if Te Pati Maori wins more electorates than its party vote share, additional seats could be added to parliament, increasing its size and the majority threshold needed to govern.

A former Samsung Electronics employee who later joined Chinese memory chipmaker ChangXin Memory Technologies, or CXMT, has been sentenced by the Seoul High Court to five years in prison for leaking Samsung's technology. This case highlights ongoing tensions in the semiconductor industry, where South Korea has designated certain manufacturing technologies as "National Core Technologies," increasing penalties for unauthorized leaks. In 2023, a South Korean court upheld Samsung's request to enforce a two-year non-compete agreement preventing a former DRAM researcher from joining US rival Micron, aiming to protect Samsung's technology and business information. Chinese semiconductor firms like CXMT have been recruiting experienced overseas engineers with attractive packages to close the technology gap. Counterpoint's Memory Tracker projects CXMT's share of global DRAM revenue could reach 10% by the second quarter of 2026. Samsung continues to monitor departing employees to discourage moves to competitors, particularly through non-compete agreements for senior staff with valuable expertise.

Arab airlines are expected to face a multi-billion dollar loss in 2026, according to projections discussed at the Arab Air Carriers’ Organization AGM held this week in Doha. The AACO, representing 39 airlines across the Middle East and North Africa, highlighted rising fuel costs, which are forecast to increase to 37.3% of operating expenses in 2026, up from 27.9% in 2025. The organisation also expressed concerns over the European Union's proposed Emissions Trading System. At the meeting, a resolution opposing the extension of the ETS to flights within 5,000 kilometres of Frankfurt Airport by 2029 was adopted. AACO warned that Arab carriers could face double costs for emissions reductions if the EU ETS and the CORSIA framework do not mutually recognise each other’s charges.

More than 250 strike actions have been announced across France to protest the high cost of living. The strikes, scheduled for November 5, 2026, involve employees and civil servants called out by unions, with young people and retirees also joining the demonstrations. Authorities have mobilised 70,000 police officers and gendarmes, promising a firm response to any disorder. Between 500,000 and 700,000 participants are expected nationwide, excluding Paris. In the capital, an alternative protest route has been set between Place de la République and Place de la Nation. The government faces limited budgetary means to address the social unrest, with the presidential election just six months away.

The UK faces a dangerous dependency on China for critical raw materials essential to its military, renewable energy infrastructure, and artificial intelligence sectors. MPs on the business select committee have called for an urgent centralised policy and the establishment of a "critical minerals finance facility" to support funding for rare earth and other critical mineral mines. Demand for lithium is expected to increase 45-fold by 2030, while the need for rare earths used in permanent magnets for military jets and other applications is projected to rise 16-fold. China currently dominates the refining of seven categories of minerals, including those vital for electric vehicles and military aircraft. This reliance poses a strategic vulnerability, especially given China's ability to abruptly halt rare earth supplies, as occurred in 2025.

NASA's Swift space telescope re-entered Earth's atmosphere over the North Sumatra region of Indonesia on November 5, 2026. The satellite, which weighed approximately 1,452 kilograms and cost $500 million, had ceased operations on October 20, 2026. A rescue attempt to raise its orbit was unsuccessful due to problems with the servicing spacecraft's attitude control. Despite the uncontrolled re-entry, there have been no immediate reports of damage on the ground. The Swift observatory had been an important tool for space observation before its mission ended.

The pound has risen to its highest overall level since the Brexit referendum over a decade ago. Sterling has strengthened notably in recent weeks against both the euro and the dollar, ahead of an expected rise in interest rates. According to Bank of England figures, the most-watched measure of the British currency's value against a basket of 18 major world currencies reached 86.4 on an index where 100 equals the value in 2005. This level is the joint highest since June 2016. The Bank of England will officially confirm this calculation on Thursday. As of 04:15 GMT on Thursday, the pound was up 0.13% against the dollar at $1.3495 and 0.16% lower against the euro at €1.1979.

David Jones, Australia's oldest department store chain, has entered voluntary administration, putting thousands of jobs at risk. The 188-year-old retailer, known locally as DJs, employs about 6,000 staff and operates 38 physical stores, including 17 in Westfield shopping centres. Voluntary administrators McGrathNicol have confirmed that both physical and online stores will continue to operate while receivers FTI Consulting review the company. This move follows years of financial struggles as customers increasingly prefer online shopping. Hours before the decision, Scentre, owner of Westfield, stated David Jones had not paid its October rent and was in breach of its tenancy agreement.

LIV Golf may be heading for liquidation after BC Partners Credit reportedly withdrew plans to finance a relaunch of the league. The investment firm had pledged up to $300 million for a revamped version, known as LIV 2.0, but pulled out after the league failed to secure enough player commitments. Only four players had publicly committed to LIV 2.0, according to Golf Digest. LIV is scheduled to appear in US bankruptcy court on Thursday, where the hearing is expected to focus on liquidation of its assets. The league filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in September after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund said it would stop funding after 2026.

Shares in NextDC surged more than 42% on Thursday after Goodman Group and U.S.-listed data centre operator Digital Realty moved to acquire stakes of up to 9.9% in the company. NextDC said the two companies conducted an aftermarket bookbuild on Wednesday, seeking shares at A$9.50 each. The NextDC board described the move as "opportunistic," noting it came two weeks after the company disclosed a power supply delay at its M3 Melbourne data centre and revised its fiscal 2027 guidance. Goodman and Digital Realty told investors they had no current intention of making a takeover bid but reserved the right to do so. The rally occurred despite weakness in U.S. data centre stocks overnight.
