Good morning. It's Friday, 20 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Russian attacks on Kyiv have resulted in the deaths of three people and caused damage to two bridges in the Ukrainian capital. This fresh wave of missile and drone strikes follows a vow of "painful" retaliation by Russian President Vladimir Putin, after Ukraine damaged the Crimea bridge earlier this week. In response to the attacks and resulting traffic difficulties between the two banks of Kyiv, the city's mayor, Vitaliy Klitschko, has urged residents to avoid using private cars to travel into the city unless absolutely necessary. President Putin has warned that Russia will respond to Ukrainian strikes on the Crimea bridge with "even more painful" attacks.

Hungary is set to lift its objections to Ukraine’s last two EU accession negotiating clusters at a meeting of EU ambassadors on Friday. This move will end Budapest’s long-standing veto that has delayed Ukraine’s and Moldova’s EU membership bids. The decision follows an agreement reached last month between Kyiv and Budapest concerning the rights of the ethnic Hungarian minority in western Ukraine, which was subsequently approved by the Ukrainian parliament. Once Hungary confirms the lifting of its veto, Ukraine and Moldova will be able to open all remaining negotiating clusters, including sensitive areas such as environment and agriculture. This development comes after the first bilateral meeting between Hungarian and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on 16 October in Brussels, signalling a warming of relations between the two countries.

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority. The court found that Myanmar security forces acted with genocidal intent during military operations in northern Rakhine state in 2017. It estimated that about 10,000 Rohingya were killed, including up to 2,109 children under the age of five. The case was brought by Gambia under the 1948 Genocide Convention, despite the country not being directly affected by the violence. The ICJ ruling requires Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those suspected of involvement in the crimes and to provide restitution and compensation. This decision recognises the atrocities committed against the Rohingya after years of denial and has brought relief, emotion, and renewed hope to Rohingya refugees who have spent nearly a decade waiting for justice.

Three candidates have officially submitted their bids for the FIFA presidency: Gianni Infantino, Philippe Diallo, and Kohzo Tashima. The election is scheduled for March 18, 2027, in Rabat, Morocco. Gianni Infantino, who has been in office for 11 years, is seeking a fourth consecutive term. Philippe Diallo, supported by UEFA, announced his challenge on Monday and is contesting Infantino's leadership. Infantino retains the backing of the Asian and African confederations. Kohzo Tashima entered the race in response to the handling of Folarin Balogun’s red card at the 2026 World Cup. Tashima has expressed willingness to coordinate with Diallo to avoid splitting the vote opposing Infantino.

Lufthansa plans to cut 1,000 jobs at its airport hubs in Frankfurt and Munich, according to a letter signed by CEO Jens Ritter and four other managers. The airline cited financial struggles as the reason behind the planned cuts. Lufthansa has operated at a €180 million loss in the first nine months of this year and is now in its third consecutive year of unprofitability. Rising fuel costs, driven by the ongoing US-Israeli war with Iran, have further complicated the airline's financial outlook.

Sean Cairncross is exiting his post as White House national cyber director after a series of technology-related outages in the United States over the past week. These outages affected both private companies and government agencies. Top US officials have sought to assure the public that there was no evidence of a cyberattack related to the outages. Cairncross has served as head of the Office of the National Cyber Director since August 2025. He was nominated by President Trump in 2025 and confirmed by the Senate to serve as his top adviser on cybersecurity and data protection policy. Cairncross has also been directly involved with the White House's deliberations over AI policy.

The Senate voted 62-33 on Thursday night to pass the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a sweeping bill to simplify and overhaul permitting rules for energy and infrastructure projects. The legislation was crafted by Senators Shelley Moore Capito, Sheldon Whitehouse, Mike Lee, and Martin Heinrich. It aims to revise and speed up environmental reviews by imposing tighter deadlines, accelerate electricity transmission, restrict federal agencies' power to revoke existing permits, and reduce chokepoints in project approvals. The bill would require new data centers of 20 megawatts or more to pay the full incremental cost of the infrastructure built to serve them. The bill now heads to the House, where Republican leadership is expected to attempt to pass it, and President Donald Trump supports the deal.

Up to 100,000 young people from the EU will be allowed to travel, work and study in the UK each year under a new youth mobility scheme expected to be agreed at the UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday. The scheme will also permit the same number of young Britons to enter Europe. Negotiators are also seeking agreements on food trade rules and carbon emissions pricing during the summit. An agreement on trade could remove checks and paperwork for food exporters to the EU, potentially adding £5 billion to the economy and reducing food prices. The UK government's request to limit numbers in the youth mobility scheme has been a sticking point in talks. If a deal is reached, legislation will be introduced to Parliament next week to implement the changes.

COP31 in Türkiye saw the launch of the ‘35 by 35’ pledge, aiming to increase the share of global energy demand met by electricity to 35% by 2035. The Asian Development Bank is leading efforts to raise about €1 billion to protect 14 Pacific island countries from rising seas and extreme weather. Four governments pledged over €180 million to the Global Environment Facility for environmental protection and climate adaptation. Canada committed up to €6.5 million to the Climate Risk and Early Warning Systems initiative. The UN-backed Global Fund for Coral Reefs announced more than €27 million for conservation in 22 developing countries. Signatories of the Paris Agreement began the second Global Stocktake, assessing progress towards climate goals. The UNEP’s methane leak monitoring system has prevented an estimated 125,000 tonnes of methane emissions since June. Finland published a national roadmap affirming its commitment to the Taskforce on Access to Climate Finance, following France, Germany, and the Netherlands.

Large demonstrations have taken place across the Czech Republic opposing Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's proposed changes to the funding model of public broadcasters. Mikuláš Minář, Chair of the Million Moments for Democracy movement, spoke about the protests, warning that the country risks following Hungary and Slovakia in democratic backsliding. These protests coincided with commemorations marking the anniversary of the 1989 Velvet Revolution. The Million Moments for Democracy movement emphasises that it is a civil movement and does not intend to participate in elections as a political party.

Ahead of a general election that may bring a right-wing government supported by the far-right Vox party, Spain's two main leaders clashed over migration, corruption, the economy, and public services. Prime Minister Sánchez accused his conservative opponent of being out of step with Europe by embracing Vox. In response, Feijóo urged Sánchez to concentrate less on international affairs and more on addressing the problems facing Spaniards at home. The debate highlighted the sharp divisions as the country prepares to vote.

Dominique de Villepin has announced his intention to run in the 2027 presidential election. The president of the movement La France humaniste made the announcement during a press conference at the Maison de l'Amérique latine in Paris on November 20, 2026. Following the conference, he planned to visit Valmondois in the Val-d'Oise. The mayor of Valmondois is Laurent de Gaulle, a descendant of General de Gaulle, who is known to be a political model for Dominique de Villepin.

Grand Theft Auto 6 has become the biggest entertainment launch in history, with pre-orders and first-day sales exceeding 24.5 million units and generating approximately $2.36 billion, according to analytics platform Sensor Tower. Around three quarters of sales were for PlayStation 5, with the remainder on Xbox. The game, which reportedly cost over $1 billion to develop, saw about 19.1 million copies sold by Thursday morning, representing $1.84 billion in sales. Its midnight release prompted many gamers to stay up all night, and Virgin Media O2 recorded its highest evening traffic as players downloaded the 212GB game file.

A 2026 Stack Overflow survey found that 83% of software developers use at least one AI tool. Gemma Rodgers, a UK-based developer, said AI has sped up software engineering and is now part of daily work for every engineer she knows. Rodgers added that the joy of overcoming challenges has diminished somewhat. James Broadhead, CTO of Taktile, noted engineers can now create ten prototypes in an afternoon instead of three weeks. Joshua Wöhle of Mindstone said AI is creating "100X engineers." Chris Sees of Hoxton Mix described AI as a capable junior engineer needing supervision. Rodgers believes challenge remains essential for accomplishment.
