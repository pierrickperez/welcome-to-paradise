Russian air strikes killed at least 15 people across the Kyiv region on 23 November 2026. An overnight strike on Dymer, about 45 kilometres north of Kyiv, hit a five-storey building, killing at least 11 people. Among the dead were a teenager and a woman in her forties, with rescuers still searching for 11 people, including two children. In Vyshneve, a Russian strike on a supermarket resulted in three deaths and 15 injuries, including three children. Additionally, a strike on a private home in Bila Tserkva killed one person and caused the building to catch fire. Rescuers managed to pull a 79-year-old woman and a child alive from the rubble in Dymer. Kyiv regional governor Tymur Tkachenko confirmed the death toll in Dymer had risen to 11.

French President Emmanuel Macron is considering adopting a 'golden rule' budgetary system to address rising debt and interest rates. According to franceinfo, a source close to the Élysée revealed this plan on Monday, November 23, 2026. Macron told Le Figaro that if financial volatility continues until January, exploring a 'golden rule' similar to that of 2011 could be a viable option. France's national debt currently stands at 3,600 billion euros. Implementing this budgetary policy would require convening the French Congress, including the National Assembly and Senate, and securing a three-fifths majority vote. The proposal has received support from the National Rally, Edouard Philippe, and Gabriel Attal, while the left remains opposed. The 'golden rule' aims to prevent a financial crisis by imposing stricter fiscal discipline amid economic uncertainty.

North Korean leader Kim Jong-un has announced his country's intention to expand its foreign relations while safeguarding national sovereignty, according to state media. Kim made the remarks during a ceremony to receive credentials from newly appointed ambassadors, including Russia's, a role traditionally performed by a senior official rather than the leader himself. The Korean Central News Agency reported that Pyongyang aims to develop foreign relations based on independence, peace, and friendship, with a focus on defending sovereignty and security. This event followed US President Donald Trump's unsuccessful attempts to arrange a meeting with Kim during his recent visit to China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Kim also commented on the current global situation, describing it as an unstable security crisis accompanied by political and economic chaos, without providing further details. Experts suggest Kim's direct involvement in the diplomatic ceremony indicates a shift in North Korea's diplomatic approach, as noted by Professor Lim Eul-chul of Kyungnam University.

The UN COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, have concluded as the longest UN climate conference in history, ending without a major new deal. The final agreement notably omitted any direct mention of fossil fuels, a point deemed unacceptable by the EU, Pacific island nations, and others. Many poorer countries expressed dissatisfaction over the lack of clear financial commitments from wealthier nations to support adaptation to climate impacts. UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres voiced deep disappointment, criticizing powerful fossil fuel interests for obstructing progress. Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, co-host of the talks, cited difficult geopolitical times as a factor in the outcome. Meanwhile, the COP31 president highlighted that over 80 countries supported Turkey's goal to increase electricity's share of global energy use to 35% by 2035.

Tata Steel is set to acquire a 26.4% stake in APL Apollo, India's largest structural steel pipe and tube maker, for 155.1 billion rupees, equivalent to 1.67 billion US dollars. The deal will increase Tata Steel's downstream integration from 35% to around 53%, according to a stock exchange filing. Following the announcement, Tata Steel shares fell nearly 5%, while APL Apollo shares dropped over 10% in early trading. Tata Steel will purchase shares from existing APL Apollo shareholders at 2,100 rupees per share and plans an open offer for an additional 26% stake at 2,301.40 rupees per share. If successful, Tata Steel would hold a controlling 52.38% stake. Tata Steel's CEO, T V Narendran, described the acquisition as creating a "downstream powerhouse" for the growing Indian market.

Officials from Syria and the European Union are negotiating a deal to normalise relations after a 15-year suspension. The agreement aims to establish a framework for trade, political dialogue, and closer cooperation. Brussels is primarily focused on the repatriation of Syrians, with over a million having fled to Europe during the Syrian war from 2011 to 2024. In January, the EU announced a two-year funding package of 620 million euros for Syria. Several European countries are considering reopening embassies or missions in Syria. Since the fall of the Assad government, around one million Syrians have returned home, mainly from neighbouring countries such as Lebanon, Turkiye, and Jordan. However, some European diplomats have expressed concern that the deal may prioritise repatriation over human rights and other critical issues, according to Al Jazeera.

Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the midterm elections, compared to at least 173 seats held by Republicans. This marks the largest Democratic majority since 1992, when the party also won 258 seats. In North Carolina, Democrat Chuck Hubbard unseated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in the 5th Congressional District, winning by 330 votes with 99% of the count complete. Foxx has represented the district since 2005. Notably, in 2024, former President Trump won this district by nearly 18 percentage points. Four House races remain undecided following the midterms.

Andy Burnham has confirmed he is not delaying plans to strengthen the UK's ties with the European Union. The Prime Minister recently promised to present options for the UK's long-term relationship with the EU, including the possibility of rejoining, later this year. Burnham described ongoing discussions with the EU as a period of "brainstorming" ideas, with a timeline spanning several months. Government officials have emphasised there is no backtracking following a "really positive" summit, instead aiming to allow the new deal to demonstrate its benefits. On Monday, Burnham will visit a Morrisons branch to highlight efforts in youth employment and promote the deal's impact on food price checks. He is also expected to brief the Commons on the summit and deal, where opposition parties, especially Reform UK, are anticipated to respond critically.

Income inequality in the European Union fell slightly in 2025, reaching its lowest level since 2010. The EU's Gini coefficient decreased to 29.2 from 29.4 in 2024, compared with an average of 30.2 over the previous 15 years. Bulgaria recorded the highest income inequality in the EU at 37.7, while Slovakia had the lowest at 23.0. Among 34 European countries, Turkey had the highest inequality with a Gini coefficient of 43.9. Romania's coefficient was 27.3, significantly below its 15-year average of 33.7. Hungary saw the largest rise, with a coefficient of 29.5, above its average of 27.6.
