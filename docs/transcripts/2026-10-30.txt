Good morning. It's Friday, 30 October. Here are the major stories from around the world.

Binance has confirmed it suffered a major hack resulting in an estimated loss of around $6.3 billion. Approximately half of the affected assets have been frozen to prevent further theft. The incident led Binance to temporarily suspend all deposits and withdrawals from early Friday. The breach is believed to have involved a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, which allowed unauthorised withdrawal transactions. Binance has pledged to cover all losses using its Secure Asset Fund for Users alongside company funds. In response, other cryptocurrency firms, including Circle and Tether, have frozen about $2.4 billion of assets linked to the hack. Following the announcement, the price of Bitcoin dropped by more than 4%. This event marks one of the largest security breaches in the cryptocurrency sector to date.

In the recent Israeli parliamentary elections, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc secured 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, falling short of the 61 needed for a majority. The opposition bloc, led by centrist Gadi Eisenkot, claimed 48 seats. The two Arab-Israeli lists, Ra'am and the Joint List, together won a historic 19 seats. Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor Eisenkot's bloc achieved an outright majority, signaling challenging coalition negotiations ahead. Netanyahu's coalition of right-wing and far-right parties remains the largest bloc but lacks the numbers to govern alone. The Arab parties, with their significant representation, could play a pivotal role as kingmakers in the formation of the next government. The final official election results are expected to be issued on November 4, 2026.

The Bank of Japan declared that underlying inflation in the country was "generally consistent" with its target of 2%, the first time it has made that assessment since adopting the target in 2013. The central bank kept its key interest rate unchanged at 1.25% after an eight-to-one majority vote, with board member Hajime Takata dissenting and calling for a rate hike to 1.5%. The BOJ upgraded its inflation forecast for fiscal year 2027 to 2.6% from 2.4%, and raised the "core-core" inflation forecast to 2.7% from 2.6%. Recent government data showed core inflation in September at 2.1%, up from 1.7% in August. The BoJ warned that the year-on-year increase in consumer prices excluding fresh food is likely to accelerate to a level clearly above 2%.

The Department of Justice has escalated its lawsuit concerning the certification of Alaska's Senate election results, citing the candidacy of a man sharing the same name as incumbent Republican Senator Dan Sullivan. President Donald Trump stated he directed the DOJ to file the suit. The amended complaint includes a new legal claim and references a New York Times article about efforts by Mary Peltola's campaign to recruit a retired schoolteacher named Dan J. Sullivan. Meanwhile, Governor Mike Dunleavy has ordered the state Department of Public Safety to investigate whether this recruitment breached any laws. In related news, President Trump has cancelled his planned rallies in Kansas and Iowa, opting instead for tele-rallies, aiming to hold more than 20 before Election Day, citing confidence in Republican Senator Roger Marshall's campaign.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for a global network of countries committed to free trade based on international rules. He made these remarks while in South Korea on the final day of his four-day trip to Asia, speaking at the Asia-Pacific Conference of German Business in Seoul. Merz said, "Competition between the US and China is ramping up. Tariffs, technological decoupling strategies and a tough race for critical raw materials and new technologies are fundamentally changing the rules of our global economy." He stated, "We are resolutely joining forces against unilateralism, autocratic power politics and violations of international law." Merz also warned that politicizing supply chains and obstructing access to key technologies jeopardizes growth, prosperity, and security of entire regions. South Korean Prime Minister Han Seong Sook said, "German companies are using Korea as a central hub to expand their presence in the Asia-Pacific region."

At least 69 people have died in a deadly rail collision on the outskirts of Urda, Spain. This tragic incident follows another crash on the same line in January, which resulted in 46 fatalities. Spain’s Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez described the occurrence of two high-speed rail disasters in less than a year as 'unacceptable' and pledged to strengthen safety measures. Meanwhile, opposition parties PP and Vox have criticised the government’s handling of the disaster. Vox has accused the government of 'killing' the victims and called for a manslaughter investigation.

Residents of Fiji's Serua Island have chosen to renovate their coastal homes rather than relocate, despite calls to move. In 2025, Serua Village collaborated with Habitat for Humanity to develop a disaster risk reduction plan, reflecting the community's preference to stay. A recent report found nearly 20% of homes are in critical condition, with only three houses strong enough to withstand a cyclone. More than half of the houses have unstable foundations, roofs, and weak structural elements, making them vulnerable to severe weather. Renovations are underway, with dozens of workers improving homes along the northern coastline, including raising some to reduce flood risk. The report also recommends enhancing access to the island, where at low tide people can walk 750 metres guided by power lines, but at high tide boats are needed, and safer docking is required.

The UK government’s pledge to reverse rising obesity rates within the next decade is at risk of failure without a significant rethink and leadership directly from the prime minister. More than a year after the promise to end the obesity epidemic, key details such as progress measurement and cross-departmental coordination remain unclear. MPs warn that tackling obesity requires top-level commitment, with a dedicated cabinet committee chaired by the prime minister to drive action. Current statistics show 30% of adults in England are living with obesity, and 66% are either overweight or obese. Nearly a third of children are also affected. The report highlights a failure to distinguish between prevention efforts and support for those already living with obesity, urging ministers to present a clear plan to parliament within three months.

Novartis CEO Vas Narasimhan told CNBC that the Swiss drugmaker expects its experimental multiple sclerosis medicine, remibrutinib, to generate more than $4 billion in peak annual sales. Barclays has cut its forecast for the drug's peak sales after two late-stage trials showed it reduced relapses in MS patients by less than analysts expected. Novartis reported sales growth this week despite losing patent protection for its heart drug Entresto. Barclays analysts note that other Novartis bestsellers, Kisqali and Kesimpta, face patent expirations around 2032, representing $18 billion in peak sales.

In the past decade, 2.6 million people in France have undergone abdominal hernia surgery, with around 250,000 operations performed annually. Pain following hernia prosthesis surgery affects between 5 and 10% of patients, and is disabling in 1 to 5% of cases. The prostheses used are typically made from polyester or polypropylene. Actor Arnaud Denis, who was euthanized in Belgium on September 22, 2026, at age 43 due to unbearable pain after hernia prosthesis surgery, had created a Facebook group in 2023. This group now has over 10,000 members sharing their experiences. At his funeral in Paris on October 1, patients applauded his coffin, acknowledging his role in raising awareness.

A federal judge has blocked a new rule from the Trump administration requiring chronically ill Medicaid recipients to prove they are "medically frail" to qualify for work exemptions. US District Judge Richard Stearns sided with 25 states and the District of Columbia, who sued over the rule in June. The rule also demanded that individuals show their conditions "significantly impair" their ability to meet work requirements. Judge Stearns cited the costly changes states would face and upheld other federal restrictions on verifying medical frailty. The work requirements themselves remain lawful.
