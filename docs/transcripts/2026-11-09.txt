Good morning. It's Monday, 9 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

A gunman suspected of killing six people in Krakow has died in hospital. The shooting began shortly before 15:00 local time in Krakow's busy Main Market Square. Six people – four women and two men – were confirmed killed in the attack. Among the victims, four were Polish nationals, while a British national and an Austrian woman were also among those killed. Following the attack, police searched the gunman's house and found three bodies – two women and one man – identified as his 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother, and 21-year-old sister. The suspect was shot within three minutes of the attack beginning, which prevented a further massacre. In total, sixteen people, including five foreigners, two police officers, and the attacker, were taken to hospital.

COP31 is underway with around 200 countries participating to maintain and strengthen global cooperation on climate change. Turkey is advocating for the '35-by-35' Global Electrification Pledge, aiming to increase electricity's share of global energy demand to 35% by 2035. Murat Kurum, COP31 president and Turkey’s Environment Minister, stated that electrification is key to achieving energy security and reducing emissions. Climate finance remains a significant topic of discussion, with particular focus on funding for adaptation and addressing loss and damage in vulnerable countries, which is expected to be a contested issue during the conference.

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his deportation policy for undocumented immigrants was "too tough" for voters, suggesting it contributed to Republican losses in the recent midterm elections. In an interview on CBS' "60 Minutes," which aired on Sunday, Mr Trump said his approach to removing undocumented immigrants who had not committed crimes was overly harsh. He indicated that mass deportations hurt his party's performance in the elections. Despite this, Mr Trump promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the mass deportation policy, to White House chief of staff on Friday. The President also expressed willingness to work with Democrats in the new Congress to craft an immigration deal, stating, "The deal would be we would work something out. And I think it's something — I think that's something that's actually doable."

Last week, hackers remotely manipulated refrigeration systems at supermarkets across Europe, forcing retailers to discard spoiled food and exposing vulnerabilities in everyday infrastructure. On Monday, supermarkets in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, and the Netherlands found that refrigeration systems had been set to as much as 50 degrees Celsius. French blood-bank refrigeration systems were subsequently targeted in what appeared to be a similar incident. A wave of suspected hoax bomb threats disrupted dozens of flights across the continent, forcing diversions and, in some cases, fighter jet escorts. The week culminated with bomb threats targeting European news outlets, including the U.K.'s Sky News and Irish public broadcaster RTÉ. Authorities have not established who was behind the attacks or whether they were connected. Defense and security experts warn that disruption to everyday services can have consequences far beyond the immediate economic damage amid ongoing geopolitical tensions in Europe.

China's producer prices rose at their fastest pace since June 2022, accelerating for the third consecutive month. The Producer Price Index for October reached 5.7%, surpassing economists' expectations of 5.3%, according to a Reuters poll. This increase was largely driven by a sharp rise in prices within the mining and raw materials sectors. Meanwhile, consumer inflation in China slowed to 0.7% in October, marking its first deceleration after two months of consecutive increases. Following the release of this data, the mainland Chinese CSI 300 index gained 0.56%.

The Netanyahu bloc holds 53 seats in the 120-member Knesset, falling short of the 61 needed to form a government. Anti-Netanyahu lawmakers control 67 seats, while Palestinian parties secured 19 seats. The ongoing corruption trial against Netanyahu is expected to last a long time. In 2020, Netanyahu broke a rotation agreement with Benny Gantz, leading to new elections which Netanyahu won. If no government is formed, new elections will be held in six months, with Netanyahu remaining in office but with limited authority.

Heavy smog has blanketed New Delhi following firecracker celebrations during Diwali, the Hindu festival of lights, which took place across India on Sunday. The Air Quality Index in several parts of the city exceeded 350 on Monday morning, categorised as "severe" and posing significant health risks. New Delhi, home to 30 million people, consistently ranks among the world's most polluted capital cities. According to India's Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, vehicles contribute 23 percent of the city's winter pollution, while biomass burning accounts for 20 percent. The firecrackers lit during Diwali have worsened the seasonal pollution crisis in the Indian capital.

Starbucks has announced plans to recruit 7,500 new baristas across the UK over the next five years. The recruitment drive will concentrate on the Midlands, northern England, Wales, and Scotland. These new hires are intended to staff 500 new coffee shops that Starbucks aims to open nationwide by 2031. Darren King, who runs Starbucks UK, said the company is committed to helping more young people access these roles by making entry-level positions as accessible as possible.

Marseille secured a 2-1 victory over Lens in the final game of Ligue 1 matchday 10. This win marks Marseille's fifth consecutive victory in the league. As a result, Olympique de Marseille now sit fourth in the Ligue 1 table.

Lando Norris won the Sao Paulo Grand Prix from pole position, finishing 14.602 seconds ahead of Max Verstappen, who impressively climbed from 21st on the grid to second place. This victory marked Norris's 14th career win and 50th podium. The race took place on a wet track, with rain at both the start and end. Mercedes secured their ninth Formula One constructors world championship at this event, needing just three points more than Ferrari to clinch the title. Lewis Hamilton finished ninth in his 400th race at the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.
