Good morning. It's Thursday, 26 November. Here are the major stories from around the world.

EU leaders met in Brussels for an extraordinary summit to discuss the future budget for the 27 member states and aid to Ukraine. The initial budget proposal for 2028 to 2034 was set at 2,000 billion euros but has been revised down several times, with the latest version cutting over 200 billion euros. Included in the budget is an additional 100 billion euros in aid for Ukraine, which remains a key point in the discussions. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has threatened to veto the budget unless all member states agree to participate in financing Ukraine. Meanwhile, the European Commission has proposed a 45 billion euro support loan, backed by joint debt, to assist Ukraine's economy and armed forces. The summit aims to reach consensus on these financial matters amid ongoing challenges in the region.

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has called for strengthening the country's nuclear arsenal and expanding the use of tactical nuclear weapons across multiple domains. His remarks came after a working visit to facilities involved in nuclear material production and weapons research. Kim linked the development of these weapons to North Korea sharing a border with what he described as its "most dangerous, hostile state," an apparent reference to South Korea. He reiterated that North Korea's status as a nuclear-armed state is irreversible, dismissing "denuclearization rhetoric" and stating that its nuclear status "will never change." Kim also accused the United States of encouraging neighbouring allies to acquire nuclear-powered submarines and pursue nuclear sharing and joint use of nuclear weapons. During his visit, Kim toured two nuclear material production plants, including one newly opened and another being expanded, as well as a nuclear-powered equipment production base and a nuclear research institute. He emphasised the urgent deployment of low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons across multiple domains, citing the border danger with South Korea.

The United States has announced a new set of tariffs affecting many of its major trading partners, including China, the European Union, and 12 other jurisdictions. China faces the highest tariff rate of up to 40%, followed by Vietnam at up to 35%, with others subject to rates of 20% or 25%. Brazil has strongly condemned the US tariff proposals, describing them as unacceptable and a unilateral action within ongoing bilateral negotiations. Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's office expressed particular concern over higher import taxes on raw and refined sugar, as well as a new 10% tariff on aircraft, which impacts the Brazilian manufacturer Embraer. The Brazilian government has not ruled out reciprocal measures in response to the tariffs. These new US tariffs could come into effect as early as January and may increase costs for US carriers purchasing Embraer jets, especially amid rising jet fuel prices.

A federal judge has blocked the Trump administration from moving to review all local broadcast licenses held by ABC. U.S. District Court Judge Loren AliKhan issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday blocking the FCC's early review of ABC licenses. The FCC began an early license review of ABC stations in April 2026, despite licenses not expiring until 2028. ABC and its corporate parent, the Walt Disney Co., serve six of the 10 largest markets in the U.S., including New York, Los Angeles, and Houston. The FCC cited concerns around ABC's diversity, equity, and inclusion efforts as the reason behind the early license renewal. Judge AliKhan wrote that the FCC chair appeared to have taken 'marching orders' from President Trump's rhetoric and stated that early license renewal proceedings create a risk sufficient to chill a broadcaster of ordinary firmness. ABC alleged that Trump and FCC Chair Brendan Carr made comments intended to chill their protected speech, escalating since the 2024 presidential election. ABC hailed the ruling as 'an important victory for the First Amendment.'

An Iraqi court is set to deliver verdicts on Thursday for 20 French nationals accused of joining the Islamic State. These men are part of a group of 47 French citizens who have been on trial in Iraq since September, having been transferred from Syria last year. The court has already sentenced 15 individuals to life imprisonment and one to 15 years in prison. Among those awaiting verdicts is Adrian Guihal, who has been linked to the 2016 Nice attack. During their hearings, most suspects pleaded guilty and expressed regret over their involvement with the group. Iraq is prosecuting them under its anti-terrorism law, which applies to those who joined IS, regardless of whether their activities took place in Iraq or Syria. In February, Iraq received over 5,700 IS detainees from more than 60 countries, transferred by the United States from Syria.

Since February 2025, over €510 billion has been invested in AI infrastructure across Malaysia, reflecting the country's growing role in the sector. By the end of 2026, around 24% of China's total computing power was sourced remotely from overseas data centres, with three-quarters located in Southeast Asia. Chinese firms rent access to US chips installed in Malaysian systems to develop AI models abroad. Alibaba Cloud operates six data centres in Malaysia, the latest opening in November 2026. In mid-2025, the US threatened a 25% tariff on Malaysian imports to prevent circumvention of chip export controls. In November 2026, the US Bureau of Industry and Security introduced new rules to stop foreign actors from using advanced computing power based on US technology.

Britain is set to invest £9.5 million in constructing a 200 megawatt windfarm to support Ukraine's energy reserves. The project, capable of powering 300,000 Ukrainian households, will be built and operated by Elementum Energy, a UK-registered company with offices in London, Kyiv, and Odesa. This initiative forms part of the UK government's broader commitment, which has already provided over £770 million to rebuild Ukraine's power system. Ed Miliband stated that the investment aims to deliver significant new renewable energy generation, enhance Ukraine's energy independence, and aid its recovery. The announcement comes amid ongoing Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, including a recent attack on a thermal power plant near Odesa.

Health expenditure data for 2024 reveals that in France, nearly 90% of medical costs are covered by Social Security and complementary health insurance, with households contributing around 10%. This out-of-pocket spending is notably lower than the European average of 16%. The figures include both public spending by Social Security and private spending by complementary health insurance, mutuals, and households. Despite this, France has fewer than four doctors per thousand inhabitants, which is less than Italy's six and Greece's slightly more than six. These statistics provide insight into the structure of healthcare financing and medical staffing across Europe.

SpaceX has announced it will donate one share of its class A stock to every active Trump Account, with CEO Elon Musk personally matching this gift. Musk stated the SpaceX shares will be transferred next month, while his personal shares will be transferred when his lockup expires in June next year. Previously, SpaceX Chief Operating Officer Gwynne Shotwell agreed to gift over 2 million shares to Trump Accounts, expected to be distributed in December. Trump Accounts, or 530A accounts, are available to any U.S. child under 18 with a Social Security number. Since their launch on July 4, contributions have exceeded $1 billion, with total deposits now over $9 billion.

Leah Blyth, a Liberal senator for South Australia, has defected to One Nation, becoming the party's first upper-house member for the state. Liberal shadow minister Anne Ruston criticised Blyth's move, saying she put her own interests ahead of voters and noted Blyth was never elected but appointed to fill a casual vacancy. One Nation deputy leader Barnaby Joyce responded to claims his party is a haven for defectors. Shadow treasurer Tim Wilson described Blyth's defection as disloyal and said he learned of it through the newspapers, like everyone else.

South Korea’s central bank has increased its key interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 3.25 percent. The Bank of Korea attributed the decision to strong domestic growth and inflation exceeding its 2 percent target, with the inflation rate at 2.8 percent in October. The country’s exports surged by 94 percent year-on-year in October, reaching $115.3 billion, largely due to booming AI chip exports. Reflecting this positive outlook, the Bank of Korea has raised its GDP growth forecasts to 3.9 percent for 2026 and 4.1 percent for 2027.
