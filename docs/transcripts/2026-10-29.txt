Good morning. It's Thursday, 29 October. Here are the major stories from around the world.

A high-speed train collision in Spain has resulted in the deaths of 68 people. The crash took place on Wednesday morning in Castilla-La Mancha, a central region south of Madrid. The incident involved a RENFE high-speed train colliding from behind with a stationary Iryo train. The RENFE train had traveled approximately 25 kilometres before the collision, which occurred after an automatic control system failed. Following the crash, Spain declared three days of national mourning starting Thursday. Transport Minister Oscar Puente attributed the accident to an "inexplicable" order from a central command centre after the security system failure. The tragedy has raised fresh concerns about rail safety and management in Spain. The collision occurred just weeks before the country's general election scheduled for November 29.

Israeli media project that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc is set to win 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, based on 91% of votes tallied. This leads the 48 seats forecast for the opposition bloc led by former military commander Gadi Eisenkot, but falls short of the 61 seats needed for a majority. The two majority Arab parties are expected to secure a record 19 seats, surpassing their previous high of 15. Many in Eisenkot's bloc have ruled out forming a government with the Palestinian-led parties projected to win these seats. Meanwhile, Israeli news website Ynet reports that just over 29,000 absentee ballots have been counted out of an estimated 500,000. These absentee ballots are for voters unable to attend their registered polling stations, including members of the IDF, diplomats, prisoners, and hospital patients. The Central Elections Commission estimates between 430,000 and 480,000 such ballots remain to be counted.

North Korea fired around a dozen short-range ballistic missiles off its east coast on Thursday. The missiles were launched from the Wonsan area towards the East Sea, also known as the Sea of Japan, flying approximately 250 kilometres. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff reported about ten missiles were fired at around 12:30pm local time. Japan's Defence Ministry confirmed the launch of what it described as a ballistic missile that fell outside Japan's Exclusive Economic Zone. The missile launches come amid rising tensions following a landmine explosion along the heavily fortified border that wounded three South Korean soldiers. This incident occurred less than a week after South Korea proposed talks over the growing border dispute. In response, South Korea's military is strengthening surveillance and maintaining readiness for additional launches, while closely sharing information with the United States and Japan.

Shell has nearly doubled its quarterly profit year-on-year to $10.81 billion, with adjusted earnings soaring from $5.43 billion a year ago. The company's indicative refining margins reached $42 per barrel, surpassing the previous record of $28.04 per barrel. Shell has announced a $5 billion share buyback programme over the next three months. CEO Wael Sawan attributed the strong operational performance to continued severe disruption in the global energy system. Crude oil prices have been highly volatile this year, influenced by the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran and attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz. Additionally, a global shortfall in refining capacity, worsened by strikes on Russian refineries, has driven up prices for gasoline, diesel and jet fuel.

Volkswagen has reported a loss of €5.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a significant increase from the €1.1 billion loss recorded in the same period last year. The company's sales fell by 6.3% to €75.3 billion during the quarter. Volkswagen is facing challenges including costs associated with transitioning to electric vehicles, declining demand, and intense competition in China. The company has also warned of a €10 billion impact on its annual profit, mainly due to the value of its stake in Porsche, which is also experiencing difficulties in China. To address these issues, Volkswagen plans to cut 120,000 jobs across the group by 2030 as part of a restructuring plan costing €31 billion. CFO Arno Antlitz stressed the importance of swift and consistent implementation of this plan.

Microsoft shares rose by 4% in extended trading following the release of its quarterly results, which surpassed expectations. The company's revenue grew by 19% year on year to $92.28 billion, exceeding the anticipated $90.69 billion. Earnings per share were reported at $4.89, above the expected $4.73. A key driver was a 48% growth in Microsoft's Azure cloud infrastructure business, which generated $33.17 billion in revenue, making it the company's largest single business segment. Despite delays from third-party providers, Azure's performance was strong. Net income increased to $36.63 billion, or $4.92 per share, up from $27.75 billion, or $3.72 per share, a year earlier. Capital expenditures and finance leases rose by about 30% to $45.2 billion during the quarter.

Tencent and Visa are expanding their partnership to enhance cross-border payments and AI-powered commerce in China. This development follows recent regulatory approvals for U.S. financial firms, including Visa and Citi, to operate in China. The companies plan to make payments easier for travellers entering and leaving China and to broaden their cross-border money transfer services. Additionally, the partnership will extend to agentic commerce, where artificial intelligence agents assist consumers in finding, selecting, and purchasing products or services. Visa received approval from the People’s Bank of China earlier this month to prepare for a domestic bank card clearing business. Tencent and Visa have collaborated since at least 2019, enabling international Visa cardholders to pay through WeChat Pay in China.

More than 250 million hectares of nature have been restored globally, according to a report published on October 29, 2026, during COP17 on biodiversity in Armenia. These restoration efforts have been recorded in 149 countries and have mainly occurred on terrestrial areas, with 232 million hectares restored. This area is comparable in size to Algeria or Argentina. China leads in terrestrial restoration, having restored 137 million hectares. The report highlights that the current pace of restoration must triple to meet the 2030 targets. Additionally, restoration efforts have created one million jobs so far, with a goal to reach 32 million by 2030.

Jio Platforms became the second most valued Indian company with a market capitalization of 13.05 trillion rupees ($137.1 billion) after its IPO. The company opened 3.3% higher on Thursday, a day after its market debut. Reliance Industries owns more than a 64% stake in Jio Platforms after the IPO. Jio Platforms has a customer base of more than half a billion, operating margins of 52%, and net leverage of 0.36 times. Mukesh Ambani became the owner of the two most valued companies in India after Jio Platforms' listing. After Jio's listing, Reliance Group's market value of 30 trillion rupees surpassed Tata Group's 25 trillion rupees, making the Ambani family business the top conglomerate in India.

Jean-Pierre Farandou, France's Minister of Labour, has said that an increase to the minimum wage, or Smic, is "not a taboo" but he currently "has no conviction" on the matter. He acknowledged the difficulties faced by French workers, noting that "the ends of the month are complicated," especially with rising fuel costs. The Minister also recognised challenges for companies amid a complicated economic period. Farandou emphasised seeking "the best possible balance" to address the issue and confirmed that the possibility of an exceptional increase demanded by unions "was discussed" but no decision has been made yet.
