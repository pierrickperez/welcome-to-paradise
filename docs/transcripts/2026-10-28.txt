Exit polls indicate a deadlock in Israel's parliamentary elections, with Benjamin Netanyahu's governing bloc and the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot each projected to win 51 seats, falling short of the 61 needed for a majority. Voter turnout reached over 77%, the highest in more than twenty years. Palestinian parties are expected to increase their Knesset representation significantly, potentially securing up to 18 seats, compared to 10 previously. With half the votes counted, Netanyahu's bloc holds 56 seats, the opposition 49, and Palestinian parties 15. Netanyahu has ruled out forming a coalition with Arab parties, a stance shared by Eisenkot and his allies. Early Wednesday, Netanyahu addressed supporters, emphasising Likud as the largest party and advocating for a strong right-wing government. Eisenkot responded by stating the public voted against Netanyahu and urged him to retire from politics.

Ukrainian forces have successfully infiltrated Russian positions near Kupiansk in a carefully planned operation known as Operation Veles. The mission involved the National Guard's 2nd Khartiia Corps and other Ukrainian units advancing through underground pipeline infrastructure. This approach was combined with simultaneous above-ground attacks and coordinated drone strikes. The operation resulted in the elimination of more than 100 Russian troops and the capture of over 30 others. The 2nd Khartiia Corps revealed details of the operation on 22 October, stating that it disrupted Russian attempts to infiltrate Ukrainian positions around Kupiansk. The city of Kupiansk, located in northeastern Ukraine, remains strategically important and has been a focal point of Russian offensive efforts. This operation has strengthened Kyiv's position in the area and inflicted significant losses on Russian forces.

President Donald Trump said Tuesday that his administration is "terminating" a Biden-era rule aimed at cutting methane emissions from oil and gas drilling. Speaking at a campaign rally in Lubbock, Texas, Trump stated that the change would save companies and consumers billions of dollars and unlock 210 million barrels of oil. He criticized the previous regulations for negatively impacting small operators. EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin previewed the changes last month, saying they would eliminate the agency's "Super Emitter Program," which requires companies to investigate and repair major methane releases from oil and natural gas facilities. The Biden methane rule was announced during a 2023 U.N. climate conference and was projected to prevent an estimated 58 million tons of methane emissions by 2038. Methane is responsible for about a third of planet-warming greenhouse gas emissions, scientists estimate. The rollback primarily affects low-producing wells, which produce about 7% to 8% of U.S. natural gas but could account for about 50% of methane leaks into the atmosphere.

Oil prices rose on Wednesday as investors considered the possibility of an extended and expanding conflict in the Middle East. This followed an attack by Tehran-backed Houthis on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery. Brent crude futures increased by $1.50, or 1.32%, reaching $115.59 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures went up by $1.47, or 1.47%, to $101.37 per barrel. The Houthis announced on Tuesday that they had targeted the Ras Tanura refinery in Saudi Arabia with a ballistic missile, raising concerns about a potential escalation in the regional conflict.

Nearly 150 researchers from over 30 countries are urging for climate change to be declared a global health emergency. According to the Lancet Countdown report, climate change caused more than half a million heat-related deaths in 2023 alone, and nearly three million since 2016—equivalent to the population of Wales. Heat waves and droughts pushed nearly 128 million more people into food insecurity in 2024 compared to the 1981–2010 average. The economic impact of heat in Asia and Africa reached 3.9% and 3.2% of GDP respectively, far higher than in Europe and the Americas. Heat wiped out 573 billion potential working hours in 2025, with farmworkers most affected. Despite this, fossil fuels still account for 80% of global energy, causing air pollution linked to 2.73 million deaths annually.

President Donald Trump held a rally at the Lubbock Memorial Civic Center in Texas to support Republican Senate candidate Ken Paxton. During the event, Trump referred to his opponent, Democratic candidate James Talarico, derisively as “Talafreako.” Trump also brought out his secretary of state, Marco Rubio, who spoke and received one of the biggest rounds of applause. On the same day, James Talarico held a separate rally in Lubbock, attracting over 4,000 attendees. At his rally, Talarico accused Ken Paxton of corruption and being a Trump sycophant. Earlier on October 27, 2026, Trump held another rally in Corpus Christi, Texas, as part of his campaign efforts.

Shares of Jio Platforms surged by 21.4% on their trading debut following a record-breaking initial public offering. The company offered 270 million shares at 1,119 rupees each, raising 302 billion rupees, equivalent to 3.2 billion US dollars, marking India's largest-ever IPO. The public issue was oversubscribed more than 22 times, with institutional investors driving demand. Akash Ambani noted that the IPO was fully subscribed within 95 minutes of opening on October 21. Shares were last trading at 1,357 rupees. Reliance Industries holds over 66% of Jio Platforms, while Meta Platforms' affiliate Jaadhu Holdings owns nearly 10%, and Google International holds 7.7%. Prominent global funds including BlackRock, Capital Group, and GIC participated in the anchor book portion of the fundraising.

Human rights groups have called on countries to reject Lana Nusseibeh's candidacy for UN secretary general, citing allegations that the UAE funds Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces. The UAE denies these accusations. Nusseibeh, 48, aims to become the first woman to lead the United Nations. Ken Roth, head of Human Rights Watch, described her as "utterly inappropriate" for the role due to her denial of the UAE's involvement with the RSF. Kate Ferguson, co-director of the British NGO Protection Approaches, raised concerns about Nusseibeh's independence from the UAE government. Nusseibeh previously served as the UAE's permanent representative to the UN from 2013 to 2024, becoming the first woman to hold that position.

An Egyptian court has ordered the closure of independent news website Mada Masr after convicting its editor-in-chief, Lina Attalah, of operating without a license. Attalah was detained for several hours following the verdict before being released after paying a fine of 3 million Egyptian pounds, the maximum penalty for the offense. Mada Masr, founded in 2013, is known for investigating corruption, human rights violations, and the military's role. The website has been blocked in Egypt since 2017, with its license application rejected since 2018. Mada Masr plans to appeal the closure order. The ruling has drawn condemnation from 119 rights organizations and media outlets including Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch, and the Committee to Protect Journalists.

The federal government plans to ban AI chatbots that create "emotional intimacy," as part of the digital duty of care bill passed by the House of Representatives on Wednesday. The bill, which will be debated in the Senate in the coming weeks, aims to require platforms to prohibit material that simulates emotional intimacy and fosters a child's dependence on it instead of human relationships. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said stories of people identifying chatbots as their best friends are a wake-up call for parliament. The bill also mandates an opt-out feature for social media algorithms and content suggestions, aiming to ensure a safe online environment for all Australians.

The French Senate has adopted and expanded the 'casseurs-payeurs' law, introducing financial solidarity for individuals who knowingly participate in groups causing damage or remain in disorderly crowds after police warnings, with a cap of 10,000 euros per person. The law, passed with 229 votes in favor and 105 against, includes new security measures such as administrative bans on protesting, increased penalties, judicial drones, algorithmic video surveillance, and evictions from social housing. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez plans to refer the law's extensions to the Council of State for review. The law follows disturbances after PSG's European championship victory, which damaged over 2,500 shops.

A community project in Sheffield is offering clothing for as little as 50p to support families struggling with the cost of living. The Echo project, based at York House in Manor Top, sells pre-loved items including coats, shoes, sportswear, and school uniforms. It is run by Manor and Castle Development Trust alongside Uni-Recycle, a community interest company that collects and redistributes school uniforms at low prices. The project also provides affordable trainers and gym wear to help people overcome barriers to physical activity. York House serves as a community hub for socialising and activities like crafting.
